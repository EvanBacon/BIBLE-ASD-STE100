# 2 Chronicles

## Chapter 1

**1** Solomon, the son of David, made his rule over the kingdom strong. The LORD his God was with him and made him very great.

**2** Solomon spoke to all Israel. He spoke to the commanders of 1,000 men and of 100 men, to the judges, and to all the leaders in Israel. These leaders were the heads of the families.

**3** Solomon and all the assembly with him went to the high place at Gibeon. The tent of meeting of God was there. Moses, the servant of the LORD, made that tent in the wilderness.

**4** But David brought the ark of God up from Kirjath-jearim to the place that he prepared for it. David put up a tent for the ark in Jerusalem.

**5** Bezaleel, the son of Uri, the son of Hur, made the bronze altar. The bronze altar was in front of the tabernacle of the LORD. Solomon and the assembly went there to ask for the help of the LORD.

**6** Solomon went up to the bronze altar in front of the LORD at the tent of meeting. He offered 1,000 burnt offerings on the altar.

**7** That night God appeared to Solomon. God said to him, "Ask for what you want me to give you."

**8** Solomon said to God, "You showed great kindness to David my father. You made me king in his place.

**9** Now, LORD God, keep your promise to David my father. You made me king over a people. The people are as many as the dust of the earth.

**10** Give me wisdom and knowledge now, so that I can lead this people. Who can judge your people, because they are so many?"

**11** God said to Solomon, "This is the wish of your heart. You did not ask for riches, possessions, or honor. You did not ask for the death of your enemies. You did not ask for a long life. But you asked for wisdom and knowledge, so that you can judge my people. I made you king over them.

**12** Thus I will give you wisdom and knowledge. I will also give you riches, possessions, and honor. No king before you had such things. No king after you will have such things."

**13** Then Solomon came back to Jerusalem from the high place at Gibeon, from the front of the tent of meeting. He ruled over Israel.

**14** Solomon collected chariots and horsemen. He had 1,400 chariots and 12,000 horsemen. He put them in the chariot cities and with the king at Jerusalem.

**15** In Jerusalem, the king made silver and gold as common as stones. He made cedar wood as common as the sycamore trees in the low hills.

**16** Solomon got horses and linen yarn from Egypt. The merchants of the king bought the linen yarn at a price.

**17** The merchants brought a chariot from Egypt for 600 shekels of silver and a horse for 150 shekels. Thus they also brought horses for all the kings of the Hittites and to the kings of Syria.

## Chapter 2

**1** Solomon decided to build a house for the name of the LORD and a palace for himself.

**2** Solomon chose 70,000 men to carry loads and 80,000 men to cut stone in the mountains. He chose 3,600 men to supervise them.

**3** Solomon sent a message to Huram, the king of Tyre. He said, "You helped David my father. You sent him cedar wood to build a house to live in. Help me in the same way.

**4** I will build a house for the name of the LORD my God. I will give the house to him. In the house we will burn sweet incense in front of him. We will put out the holy bread at all times. We will offer burnt offerings in the morning and in the evening. We will do this on the Sabbaths, at the new moons, and at the holy feasts of the LORD our God. This is a permanent law for Israel.

**5** The house that I build will be great, because our God is greater than all gods.

**6** But who can build a house for God? The heavens, even the highest heavens, cannot hold him. Who am I to build a house for him? I can only build a place to burn sacrifices in front of him.

**7** Thus, send me a man with skill to work in gold, silver, bronze, and iron. He must also know how to work in purple, red, and blue cloth. He must know how to cut designs. He will work with the skilled men that are with me in Judah and in Jerusalem. David my father supplied these men.

**8** Also send me cedar, pine, and algum logs from Lebanon. I know that your servants have skill to cut trees in Lebanon. My servants will work with your servants.

**9** They will prepare much wood for me, because the house that I will build will be large and wonderful.

**10** I will give your servants, the men who cut the trees, 20,000 measures of crushed wheat and 20,000 measures of barley. I will also give them 20,000 baths of wine and 20,000 baths of olive oil."

**11** Then Huram, the king of Tyre, wrote a letter and sent it to Solomon. Huram wrote, "The LORD loves his people. Thus he made you king over them."

**12** Huram also wrote, "Praise the LORD, the God of Israel, who made heaven and earth. He gave King David a wise son. This son has good judgment and understanding. He will build a house for the LORD and a palace for himself.

**13** Now I send you a skilled man with much understanding. His name is Huram-abi.

**14** His mother is from the tribe of Dan, and his father is a man from Tyre. He has skill to work in gold, silver, bronze, iron, stone, and wood. He can work in purple, blue, and red cloth and in fine linen. He can cut all types of designs. He can make each plan that you give him. He will work with your skilled men and with the skilled men of my lord David, your father.

**15** Now, my lord, send to your servants the wheat, the barley, the olive oil, and the wine that you promised.

**16** We will cut all the wood from Lebanon that you need. We will tie the logs together and float them by sea to Joppa. Then you can take the wood up to Jerusalem."

**17** Solomon counted all the foreigners in the land of Israel. He did this after the count that David his father made. There were 153,600 foreigners.

**18** He made 70,000 of them carry loads. He made 80,000 of them cut stone in the mountains. He made 3,600 of them supervisors to make the people work.

## Chapter 3

**1** Then Solomon started to build the house of the LORD at Jerusalem on Mount Moriah. The LORD appeared to David his father there. David prepared that place on the threshing floor of Ornan the Jebusite.

**2** Solomon started to build on the second day of the second month, in the fourth year of his rule.

**3** These are the measurements that Solomon used for the house of God. With the old standard of cubits, the house was 60 cubits long and 20 cubits wide.

**4** The porch at the front of the house was 20 cubits long, the same as the width of the house. The porch was 120 cubits high. Solomon covered the inside of the porch with pure gold.

**5** He covered the main room with pine wood and then covered the wood with fine gold. He put designs of palm trees and chains on it.

**6** He put valuable stones in the house to make it beautiful. The gold was from Parvaim.

**7** He covered the house with gold. He covered the beams, the doorposts, the walls, and the doors with gold. He cut figures of cherubim (angels with wings) on the walls.

**8** He made the most holy room. It was 20 cubits long, the same as the width of the house, and 20 cubits wide. He covered it with 600 talents of fine gold.

**9** The nails weighed 50 shekels of gold. He also covered the upper rooms with gold.

**10** In the most holy room he made 2 statues of cherubim and covered them with gold.

**11** The wings of the cherubim were 20 cubits long in total. One wing of the first cherub was 5 cubits long and touched the wall of the house. The other wing was also 5 cubits long and touched the wing of the second cherub.

**12** One wing of the second cherub was 5 cubits long and touched the other wall of the house. The other wing was also 5 cubits long and touched the wing of the first cherub.

**13** The wings of these cherubim spread out 20 cubits. The cherubim stood on their feet, and their faces were toward the main room.

**14** He made the curtain from blue, purple, and red cloth and from fine linen. He put designs of cherubim on the curtain.

**15** In front of the house he made 2 pillars, 35 cubits high. On the top of each pillar there was a capital 5 cubits high.

**16** He made chains, as in the inner room, and put them on the tops of the pillars. He made 100 pomegranates and put them on the chains.

**17** He put up the pillars in front of the temple, one on the right side and one on the left side. He gave the name Jachin to the pillar on the right. He gave the name Boaz to the pillar on the left.

## Chapter 4

**1** He made a bronze altar. It was 20 cubits long, 20 cubits wide, and 10 cubits high.

**2** He also made a large round tank of metal, called the Sea. It was 10 cubits from rim to rim and 5 cubits high. The distance around it was 30 cubits.

**3** Below the rim there were figures of oxen around the Sea, 10 for each cubit. The workers made 2 rows of oxen in the same casting as the Sea.

**4** The Sea stood on 12 oxen. 3 oxen looked north, 3 looked west, 3 looked south, and 3 looked east. The Sea was on top of them, and the back parts of the oxen were toward the center.

**5** The metal of the Sea was the width of a hand thick. Its rim was like the rim of a cup, with the shape of a lily flower. The Sea held 3,000 baths of water.

**6** He also made 10 basins for washing. He put 5 on the right side and 5 on the left side. The priests washed the parts of the burnt offerings in them. But the priests used the Sea to wash themselves.

**7** He made 10 gold lampstands, as the plan told him. He put them in the temple, 5 on the right side and 5 on the left side.

**8** He also made 10 tables and put them in the temple, 5 on the right side and 5 on the left side. He made 100 gold bowls.

**9** He made the court of the priests, the large court, and the doors for the court. He covered the doors with bronze.

**10** He put the Sea on the right side of the house, at the southeast corner.

**11** Huram also made the pots, the shovels, and the bowls. Thus Huram completed the work that he did for King Solomon in the house of God:

**12** the 2 pillars, the 2 bowl-shaped capitals on the tops of the pillars, and the 2 networks of chains to cover the 2 capitals on the tops of the pillars;

**13** the 400 pomegranates for the 2 networks, 2 rows of pomegranates for each network, to cover the 2 bowl-shaped capitals on the pillars;

**14** the stands, and the basins on the stands;

**15** the Sea, and the 12 oxen under it;

**16** the pots, the shovels, the meat forks, and all the related tools. Huram-abi made all these items of polished bronze for King Solomon, for the house of the LORD.

**17** The king made the castings in clay molds in the plain of the Jordan, between Succoth and Zeredathah.

**18** Solomon made very many of these items. Nobody could know the weight of the bronze.

**19** Solomon also made all the items for the house of God. He made the gold altar and the tables for the holy bread.

**20** He made the lampstands and their lamps of pure gold. The lamps burned in front of the inner room, as the law told.

**21** He made the flowers, the lamps, and the tongs of gold, the purest gold.

**22** He made the wick cutters, the bowls, the spoons, and the fire pans of pure gold. The entrance of the house, the inner doors to the most holy place, and the doors of the main room of the temple were of gold.

## Chapter 5

**1** Thus Solomon completed all the work for the house of the LORD. Then Solomon brought in the items that David his father gave to God. He put the silver, the gold, and all the items in the treasuries of the house of God.

**2** Then Solomon called the elders of Israel to Jerusalem. He called all the heads of the tribes and the leaders of the families of Israel. They came to bring the ark of the covenant of the LORD up from the City of David, which is Zion.

**3** All the men of Israel came to the king at the feast in the seventh month.

**4** All the elders of Israel came, and the Levites lifted up the ark.

**5** They brought up the ark, the tent of meeting, and all the holy items in the tent. The priests and the Levites carried them up.

**6** King Solomon and all the assembly of Israel with him stood in front of the ark. They sacrificed sheep and oxen. There were so many animals that nobody could count them.

**7** The priests brought the ark of the covenant of the LORD to its place in the inner room of the house. They put it in the most holy place, under the wings of the cherubim.

**8** The cherubim spread their wings over the place of the ark. Thus the cherubim covered the ark and its carrying poles from above.

**9** The poles were very long. People could see the ends of the poles from the front of the inner room, but not from outside. The poles are there to this day.

**10** There was nothing in the ark but the 2 stone tablets. Moses put them there at Horeb. At Horeb the LORD made a covenant with the people of Israel after they came out of Egypt.

**11** Then the priests came out of the holy place. All the priests that were there made themselves holy. They did this without attention to their divisions.

**12** All the Levites who were singers stood at the east side of the altar. These were Asaph, Heman, Jeduthun, and their sons and relatives. They wore fine linen and played cymbals, lyres, and harps. With them, 120 priests blew trumpets.

**13** The trumpet players and the singers made one sound together to praise and thank the LORD. They sang loudly with trumpets, cymbals, and other instruments. They praised the LORD and sang, "He is good. His love continues forever." Then a cloud filled the house of the LORD.

**14** Because of the cloud, the priests could not stand to do their work. The glory of the LORD filled the house of God.

## Chapter 6

**1** Then Solomon said, "The LORD said that he will live in thick darkness.

**2** But I built a great house for you. It is a place for you to live in forever."

**3** Then the king turned around and blessed all the assembly of Israel. All the assembly of Israel stood.

**4** He said, "Praise the LORD, the God of Israel. With his power he did the thing that he promised to my father David. He said,

**5** 'Since the day that I brought my people out of the land of Egypt, I did not choose a city in a tribe of Israel to build a house for my name. I did not choose a man to rule my people Israel.

**6** But now I chose Jerusalem as the place for my name. I chose David to rule my people Israel.'

**7** David my father wanted to build a house for the name of the LORD, the God of Israel.

**8** But the LORD said to David my father, 'You wanted to build a house for my name. It was good that you wanted to do this.

**9** But you will not build the house. Your son, who will be born to you, will build the house for my name.'

**10** Now the LORD did the thing that he promised. I became king after David my father. I sit on the throne of Israel, as the LORD promised. I built the house for the name of the LORD, the God of Israel.

**11** I put the ark in the house. In the ark is the covenant that the LORD made with the people of Israel."

**12** Then Solomon stood in front of the altar of the LORD, in front of all the assembly of Israel. He lifted up his hands.

**13** Solomon made a bronze platform, 5 cubits long, 5 cubits wide, and 3 cubits high. He put it in the center of the court. He stood on it. Then he went down on his knees in front of all the assembly of Israel. He lifted his hands toward heaven.

**14** He said, "LORD, God of Israel, there is no God like you in heaven or on earth. You keep your covenant. You show love to your servants who obey you with all their heart.

**15** You kept your promise to your servant David, my father. You spoke the promise, and with your power you made it true today.

**16** Now, LORD, God of Israel, keep the other promise to your servant David, my father. You said, 'A man from your family will always sit on the throne of Israel. But your sons must be careful to obey my law, as you obeyed me.'

**17** Now, LORD, God of Israel, make true the word that you spoke to your servant David.

**18** But will God really live on the earth with people? The heavens, even the highest heavens, cannot hold you. Thus this house that I built cannot hold you!

**19** But, LORD my God, give attention to the prayer of your servant and to his request. Hear the cry and the prayer that your servant prays to you.

**20** Watch over this house day and night. You said that you will put your name in this place. Hear the prayer that your servant prays toward this place.

**21** Hear the requests of your servant and of your people Israel when they pray toward this place. Hear from heaven, the place where you live. When you hear, forgive.

**22** A man can do wrong against his neighbor. Then the man must make an oath. He can come and make the oath in front of your altar in this house.

**23** Then hear from heaven and act. Judge your servants. Punish the guilty man, and give him what he deserves. Show that the innocent man is innocent, and reward him for his good actions.

**24** Your people Israel can sin against you, and an enemy can defeat them. Then they can come back to you and praise your name. They can pray to you in this house and ask for your help.

**25** Then hear from heaven. Forgive the sin of your people Israel. Bring them back to the land that you gave to them and to their ancestors.

**26** Your people can sin against you, and you can stop the rain. Then they can pray toward this place and praise your name. They can turn from their sin because you punished them.

**27** Then hear from heaven. Forgive the sin of your servants, your people Israel. Teach them the good way to live. Send rain on your land. You gave this land to your people as their possession.

**28** There can be famine or disease in the land. There can be crop disease, mold, locusts, or grasshoppers. Enemies can attack the cities of the land. There can be any disaster or any sickness.

**29** Then any person or all your people Israel can pray to you. Each person knows his own pain and grief. He can lift up his hands toward this house.

**30** Then hear from heaven, the place where you live, and forgive. Give each person what he deserves for all his actions. You know his heart. Only you know the hearts of all people.

**31** Then they will fear you and obey you all the days that they live in the land. You gave this land to our ancestors.

**32** A foreigner who is not from your people Israel can come from a far country. He will come because of your great name and your great power. He can come and pray toward this house.

**33** Then hear from heaven, the place where you live. Do all the things that the foreigner asks from you. Then all the peoples of the earth will know your name. They will fear you, as your people Israel fear you. They will know that this house that I built has your name.

**34** Your people can go to war against their enemies on the road where you send them. They can pray to you toward this city that you chose and toward the house that I built for your name.

**35** Then hear their prayer and their request from heaven, and give them victory.

**36** Your people can sin against you, because all people sin. You can become angry with them and give them to an enemy. The enemy can take them as prisoners to a land far away or near.

**37** In that land, your people can change their minds. They can turn back to you and pray in the land of their captivity. They can say, 'We sinned. We did wrong. We did evil.'

**38** They can come back to you with all their heart and with all their soul in that land. They can pray toward the land that you gave to their ancestors. They can pray toward the city that you chose and toward the house that I built for your name.

**39** Then hear their prayer and their requests from heaven, the place where you live. Help them. Forgive your people who sinned against you.

**40** Now, my God, watch and listen to the prayers that people pray in this place.

**41** Now, LORD God, go up to your resting place, you and the ark of your power. LORD God, let your priests wear salvation as clothes. Let your faithful people be happy because of your goodness.

**42** LORD God, do not turn away from your anointed king. Remember your love for David your servant."

## Chapter 7

**1** When Solomon stopped his prayer, fire came down from heaven. The fire burned the burnt offering and the sacrifices. The glory of the LORD filled the house.

**2** The priests could not go into the house of the LORD, because the glory of the LORD filled the house of the LORD.

**3** All the people of Israel saw the fire come down and the glory of the LORD on the house. They went down with their faces to the ground on the stone floor. They worshiped and thanked the LORD. They said, "He is good. His love continues forever."

**4** Then the king and all the people offered sacrifices to the LORD.

**5** King Solomon offered a sacrifice of 22,000 oxen and 120,000 sheep. Thus the king and all the people gave the house of God to God.

**6** The priests stood at their posts. The Levites stood with the instruments of music for the LORD. King David made these instruments to thank the LORD, because his love continues forever. David used them when he praised God with the Levites. The priests blew trumpets in front of the Levites, and all Israel stood.

**7** Solomon made the center of the court in front of the house of the LORD holy. There he offered burnt offerings and the fat of the peace offerings. He did this because the bronze altar that he made was too small. It could not hold the burnt offerings, the grain offerings, and the fat.

**8** At that time Solomon held the feast for 7 days, and all Israel with him. It was a very large assembly. The people came from the road to Hamath in the north to the brook of Egypt in the south.

**9** On the eighth day they held a holy assembly. They held the dedication of the altar for 7 days and the feast for 7 days.

**10** On the twenty-third day of the seventh month, Solomon sent the people to their homes. They were glad and happy in their hearts. They were happy because of the good things that the LORD did for David, for Solomon, and for his people Israel.

**11** Thus Solomon completed the house of the LORD and the palace of the king. He did well all the things that he wanted to do in the house of the LORD and in his own palace.

**12** Then the LORD appeared to Solomon at night. The LORD said to him, "I heard your prayer. I chose this place for myself as a house of sacrifice.

**13** I can stop the rain from the sky. I can command the locusts to eat the crops of the land. I can send disease among my people.

**14** Then my people, who have my name, must make themselves humble and pray. They must look for me and turn from their evil ways. Then I will hear from heaven. I will forgive their sin and I will heal their land.

**15** Now I will watch and listen to the prayers that people pray in this place.

**16** Now I chose this house and made it holy, so that my name will be there forever. My eyes and my heart will be there always.

**17** You must obey me as David your father obeyed me. Do all that I command you. Obey my laws and my rules.

**18** Then I will make your royal throne strong. I made this covenant with David your father. I said, 'A man from your family will always rule Israel.'

**19** But you and your people can turn away from me. You can stop obeying my laws and my commands that I gave you. You can go and serve other gods and worship them.

**20** If you do these things, then I will pull my people out of my land that I gave them. I will reject this house that I made holy for my name. All nations will laugh at this house and use it as an example of disaster.

**21** This house is now great. But each person who goes past it will be shocked. He will say, 'Why did the LORD do this to this land and to this house?'

**22** People will answer, 'They left the LORD, the God of their ancestors, who brought them out of the land of Egypt. They took other gods, worshiped them, and served them. Thus the LORD brought all this disaster on them.'"

## Chapter 8

**1** After 20 years, Solomon completed the house of the LORD and his own palace.

**2** Then Solomon built again the cities that Huram gave to him. He made the people of Israel live there.

**3** Solomon went to Hamath-zobah and took control of it.

**4** He built Tadmor in the wilderness. He built all the store cities in Hamath.

**5** He also built Upper Beth-horon and Lower Beth-horon. They were strong cities with walls, gates, and bars.

**6** He also built Baalath and all his store cities. He built all the cities for his chariots and his horsemen. He built all the things that he wanted to build in Jerusalem, in Lebanon, and in all the land that he ruled.

**7** Some people of the Hittites, the Amorites, the Perizzites, the Hivites, and the Jebusites stayed in the land. These people were not from Israel.

**8** They were the descendants of the people that the Israelites did not kill. Solomon made these descendants do forced work, and they do this work to this day.

**9** But Solomon did not make slaves of the people of Israel for his work. The Israelites were his soldiers, his chief officers, and the commanders of his chariots and his horsemen.

**10** King Solomon also had 250 chief officers. These officers ruled over the people.

**11** Solomon brought the daughter of Pharaoh up from the City of David to the palace that he built for her. He said, "My wife must not live in the palace of David, king of Israel. The places where the ark of the LORD came are holy."

**12** Then Solomon offered burnt offerings to the LORD on the altar of the LORD. He built this altar in front of the porch.

**13** He offered the sacrifices that the law of Moses told for each day. He offered them on the Sabbaths, at the new moons, and at the 3 yearly feasts. These feasts are the Feast of Unleavened Bread, the Feast of Weeks, and the Feast of Shelters.

**14** Solomon obeyed the rules of David his father. He put the priests into their divisions for their work. He put the Levites into their positions to praise God and to help the priests with the work of each day. He also put the gatekeepers into their divisions at each gate. David, the man of God, commanded these things.

**15** The priests and the Levites obeyed all the commands of the king. They obeyed in all things, also in the work of the treasuries.

**16** Thus all the work of Solomon was ready, from the day of the foundation of the house of the LORD until the end. Thus the house of the LORD was complete.

**17** Then Solomon went to Ezion-geber and to Eloth, on the coast of the sea in the land of Edom.

**18** Huram sent ships to him with his servants. These servants knew the sea well. They went with the servants of Solomon to Ophir. There they got 450 talents of gold and brought it to King Solomon.

## Chapter 9

**1** The queen of Sheba heard about the fame of Solomon. She came to Jerusalem to test Solomon with difficult questions. She came with a very large group of people. Her camels carried spices, much gold, and valuable stones. She came to Solomon and talked with him about all the things in her mind.

**2** Solomon answered all her questions. No question was too difficult for Solomon to explain to her.

**3** The queen of Sheba saw the wisdom of Solomon and the palace that he built.

**4** She saw the food on his table and the seats of his officials. She saw his servants and their clothes. She saw his cupbearers and their clothes. She saw the burnt offerings that he offered at the house of the LORD. She was very surprised.

**5** She said to the king, "In my own land I heard about your actions and your wisdom. The report was true.

**6** But I did not believe the report until I came and saw it with my eyes. Nobody told me even half of your great wisdom. You are greater than the report that I heard.

**7** Your men are happy! These servants of yours are happy! They always stand in front of you and hear your wisdom.

**8** Praise the LORD your God! He was pleased with you and put you on his throne as king for the LORD your God. Your God loves Israel and will make Israel strong forever. Thus he made you king over them. You will do what is fair and right."

**9** Then she gave the king 120 talents of gold, very many spices, and valuable stones. Nobody ever gave King Solomon spices like the spices from the queen of Sheba.

**10** The servants of Huram and the servants of Solomon brought gold from Ophir. They also brought algum wood and valuable stones.

**11** The king used the algum wood to make steps for the house of the LORD and for the palace of the king. He also made harps and lyres for the singers. Nobody saw such things before in the land of Judah.

**12** King Solomon gave the queen of Sheba all the things that she wanted and asked for. He gave her more than she brought to him. Then she and her servants went back to her own land.

**13** The weight of the gold that Solomon got in one year was 666 talents.

**14** This was in addition to the gold from the traders and the merchants. All the kings of Arabia and the governors of the land also brought gold and silver to Solomon.

**15** King Solomon made 200 large shields of hammered gold. He used 600 shekels of hammered gold for each shield.

**16** He also made 300 small shields of hammered gold. He used 300 shekels of gold for each shield. The king put them in the Palace of the Forest of Lebanon.

**17** The king also made a large throne of ivory and covered it with pure gold.

**18** The throne had 6 steps and a gold footstool. The steps and the footstool were attached to the throne. There were armrests on each side of the seat. 2 lions stood next to the armrests.

**19** 12 lions stood on the 6 steps, one at each end of each step. Nobody made a throne like this in any other kingdom.

**20** All the cups of King Solomon were of gold. All the items in the Palace of the Forest of Lebanon were of pure gold. None were of silver, because people did not think that silver was valuable in the time of Solomon.

**21** The ships of the king went to Tarshish with the servants of Huram. Each 3 years the ships of Tarshish came back. They brought gold, silver, ivory, apes, and peacocks.

**22** King Solomon was greater than all the kings of the earth in riches and wisdom.

**23** All the kings of the earth wanted to meet Solomon. They wanted to hear the wisdom that God put in his heart.

**24** Each year, each king brought a gift. They brought items of silver and gold, clothes, weapons, spices, horses, and mules.

**25** Solomon had 4,000 stalls for horses and chariots, and 12,000 horsemen. He kept them in the chariot cities and with the king at Jerusalem.

**26** He ruled over all the kings from the Euphrates River to the land of the Philistines and to the border of Egypt.

**27** In Jerusalem, the king made silver as common as stones. He made cedar wood as common as the sycamore trees in the low hills.

**28** People brought horses to Solomon from Egypt and from all other lands.

**29** The other actions of Solomon, from the start to the end, are in the records of Nathan the prophet. They are also in the prophecy of Ahijah the Shilonite and in the visions of Iddo the seer about Jeroboam, the son of Nebat.

**30** Solomon ruled in Jerusalem over all Israel for 40 years.

**31** Then Solomon died, and the people buried him in the City of David his father. Rehoboam his son became king after him.

## Chapter 10

**1** Rehoboam went to Shechem, because all Israel went to Shechem to make him king.

**2** Jeroboam, the son of Nebat, was in Egypt. He went there to escape from King Solomon. Jeroboam heard the news and came back from Egypt.

**3** The people sent for Jeroboam. Then Jeroboam and all Israel came and spoke to Rehoboam. They said,

**4** "Your father made our work very hard. Now make the hard work and the heavy load from your father lighter. Then we will serve you."

**5** Rehoboam said to them, "Come back to me after 3 days." Then the people went away.

**6** King Rehoboam asked for advice from the old men. These men served Solomon his father while Solomon was alive. Rehoboam said, "What answer do you tell me to give to these people?"

**7** They said to him, "Be kind to these people. Please them and speak good words to them. Then they will be your servants forever."

**8** But Rehoboam did not accept the advice of the old men. He asked for advice from the young men who grew up with him. These young men served him.

**9** He said to them, "What answer do you tell me to give to these people? They said to me, 'Make the load that your father put on us lighter.'"

**10** The young men who grew up with him said to him, "The people said to you, 'Your father made our load heavy, but make it lighter for us.' Tell them this: 'My little finger is thicker than the waist of my father.

**11** My father put a heavy load on you, but I will make your load heavier. My father punished you with whips, but I will punish you with scorpions.'"

**12** On the third day, Jeroboam and all the people came back to Rehoboam. The king told them, "Come back to me on the third day."

**13** The king answered them roughly. King Rehoboam did not accept the advice of the old men.

**14** He used the advice of the young men. He said, "My father made your load heavy, but I will make it heavier. My father punished you with whips, but I will punish you with scorpions."

**15** Thus the king did not listen to the people. God caused this to occur. Thus the LORD made true the word that he spoke to Jeroboam, the son of Nebat, through Ahijah the Shilonite.

**16** All Israel saw that the king did not listen to them. Then the people answered the king, "We have no share in David! We have no possession in the son of Jesse! Israel, all of you go to your homes! David, now take care of your own family!" Then all the Israelites went to their homes.

**17** But Rehoboam ruled over the Israelites who lived in the cities of Judah.

**18** Then King Rehoboam sent Hadoram, who supervised the forced workers. The people of Israel threw stones at Hadoram, and he died. King Rehoboam quickly got into his chariot and ran away to Jerusalem.

**19** Thus Israel rebelled against the family of David, and this continues to this day.

## Chapter 11

**1** Rehoboam came to Jerusalem. He collected 180,000 chosen soldiers from the tribes of Judah and Benjamin. He wanted to fight against Israel and get the kingdom back.

**2** But the word of the LORD came to Shemaiah, the man of God. The LORD said,

**3** "Speak to Rehoboam, the son of Solomon, king of Judah. Also speak to all the Israelites in Judah and Benjamin. Tell them,

**4** 'The LORD says this: Do not go up to fight against your relatives. Go back home, each man. I caused this thing to occur.'" They obeyed the words of the LORD. They did not go to attack Jeroboam.

**5** Rehoboam lived in Jerusalem. He built cities in Judah for defense.

**6** He built Bethlehem, Etam, Tekoa,

**7** Beth-zur, Shoco, Adullam,

**8** Gath, Mareshah, Ziph,

**9** Adoraim, Lachish, Azekah,

**10** Zorah, Aijalon, and Hebron. These were strong cities in Judah and in Benjamin.

**11** He made the forts stronger. He put commanders in them. He put supplies of food, olive oil, and wine in them.

**12** He put shields and spears in each city and made the cities very strong. Thus Judah and Benjamin stayed with him.

**13** The priests and the Levites in all Israel came to him from all their areas.

**14** The Levites left their pastures and their land and came to Judah and Jerusalem. They came because Jeroboam and his sons did not let them serve as priests of the LORD.

**15** Jeroboam chose his own priests for the high places, for the goat idols, and for the calf idols that he made.

**16** People from all the tribes of Israel followed the Levites to Jerusalem. These people wanted with all their heart to worship the LORD, the God of Israel. They came to sacrifice to the LORD, the God of their ancestors.

**17** They made the kingdom of Judah strong. They supported Rehoboam, the son of Solomon, for 3 years. For 3 years they lived in the way of David and Solomon.

**18** Rehoboam married Mahalath. She was the daughter of Jerimoth, the son of David, and of Abihail, the daughter of Eliab, the son of Jesse.

**19** Mahalath gave birth to sons for him: Jeush, Shamariah, and Zaham.

**20** After her, he married Maachah, the daughter of Absalom. Maachah gave birth to Abijah, Attai, Ziza, and Shelomith.

**21** Rehoboam loved Maachah, the daughter of Absalom, more than all his other wives and concubines. He had 18 wives and 60 concubines. He became the father of 28 sons and 60 daughters.

**22** Rehoboam made Abijah, the son of Maachah, the chief prince among his brothers. He wanted to make Abijah king.

**23** Rehoboam acted wisely. He sent some of his sons to all the areas of Judah and Benjamin, to all the strong cities. He gave them much food. He also found many wives for them.

## Chapter 12

**1** Rehoboam made his kingdom firm and became strong. Then he stopped obeying the law of the LORD, and all Israel stopped with him.

**2** In the fifth year of King Rehoboam, Shishak, the king of Egypt, attacked Jerusalem. This occurred because the people were not faithful to the LORD.

**3** Shishak had 1,200 chariots and 60,000 horsemen. Nobody could count the soldiers who came with him from Egypt. These were Libyans, Sukkiim, and Ethiopians.

**4** Shishak took the strong cities of Judah and came to Jerusalem.

**5** Then Shemaiah the prophet came to Rehoboam and to the leaders of Judah. These leaders were together in Jerusalem because of Shishak. Shemaiah said to them, "The LORD says this: 'You left me. Thus I now leave you in the power of Shishak.'"

**6** Then the leaders of Israel and the king made themselves humble. They said, "The LORD is right."

**7** The LORD saw that they made themselves humble. Then the word of the LORD came to Shemaiah: "They made themselves humble. Thus I will not destroy them. I will soon save them. I will not use Shishak to pour my anger on Jerusalem.

**8** But they will become his servants. Then they will know the difference between my service and the service of the kings of other lands."

**9** Shishak, the king of Egypt, attacked Jerusalem. He took the treasures of the house of the LORD and the treasures of the palace of the king. He took all of them. He also took the gold shields that Solomon made.

**10** King Rehoboam made bronze shields to replace the gold shields. He gave them to the commanders of the guards who guarded the entrance of the palace of the king.

**11** Each time the king went into the house of the LORD, the guards carried the shields. Then they brought the shields back to the guardroom.

**12** Rehoboam made himself humble. Thus the anger of the LORD turned away from him, and the LORD did not destroy him completely. There was also some good in Judah.

**13** King Rehoboam made himself strong in Jerusalem and continued to rule. Rehoboam was 41 years old when he became king. He ruled for 17 years in Jerusalem. The LORD chose Jerusalem from all the tribes of Israel as the place for his name. The name of the mother of Rehoboam was Naamah. She was an Ammonite.

**14** Rehoboam did evil, because he did not decide in his heart to obey the LORD.

**15** The actions of Rehoboam, from the start to the end, are in the records of Shemaiah the prophet and of Iddo the seer. These records include the family lists. There was war all the time between Rehoboam and Jeroboam.

**16** Then Rehoboam died, and the people buried him in the City of David. Abijah his son became king after him.

## Chapter 13

**1** In the eighteenth year of King Jeroboam, Abijah became king over Judah.

**2** He ruled for 3 years in Jerusalem. The name of his mother was Michaiah, the daughter of Uriel of Gibeah. There was war between Abijah and Jeroboam.

**3** Abijah went to battle with an army of 400,000 brave chosen soldiers. Jeroboam prepared for battle against him with 800,000 chosen strong soldiers.

**4** Abijah stood on Mount Zemaraim in the hill country of Ephraim. He said, "Jeroboam and all Israel, listen to me!

**5** You must know that the LORD, the God of Israel, gave the rule over Israel to David and his sons forever. He did this with a covenant that cannot be broken.

**6** But Jeroboam, the son of Nebat, rebelled against his master. Jeroboam was the servant of Solomon, the son of David.

**7** Worthless and evil men joined Jeroboam. They opposed Rehoboam, the son of Solomon. Rehoboam was young and weak, and he could not fight against them.

**8** Now you think that you can fight against the kingdom of the LORD. The LORD gave this kingdom to the sons of David. You have a very large army. You have with you the gold calves that Jeroboam made as your gods.

**9** You sent away the priests of the LORD, the sons of Aaron, and the Levites. You made your own priests, as the peoples of other lands do. Any man can come with a young bull and 7 rams to become a priest. Then he becomes a priest of gods that are not real gods.

**10** But the LORD is our God, and we did not leave him. The priests who serve the LORD are the sons of Aaron. The Levites help them in their work.

**11** Each morning and each evening they offer burnt offerings and sweet incense to the LORD. They put the holy bread on the clean table. They light the lamps on the gold lampstand each evening. We obey the commands of the LORD our God, but you left him.

**12** Look, God is with us as our leader. His priests are ready to blow their trumpets to give the signal for battle against you. People of Israel, do not fight against the LORD, the God of your ancestors. You will not win."

**13** But Jeroboam sent some soldiers around to attack Judah from behind. Thus his army was in front of Judah, and the hidden soldiers were behind Judah.

**14** The men of Judah turned and saw the enemy in front of them and behind them. They cried out to the LORD, and the priests blew the trumpets.

**15** Then the men of Judah shouted a battle cry. When they shouted, God defeated Jeroboam and all Israel in front of Abijah and Judah.

**16** The Israelites ran away from Judah, and God gave them into the power of Judah.

**17** Abijah and his army killed very many of them. 500,000 chosen men of Israel died.

**18** Thus the people of Israel lost at that time. The people of Judah won, because they trusted the LORD, the God of their ancestors.

**19** Abijah chased Jeroboam and took cities from him. He took Bethel, Jeshanah, and Ephrain, with their villages.

**20** Jeroboam did not become strong again while Abijah lived. Then the LORD struck Jeroboam, and he died.

**21** But Abijah became strong. He married 14 wives. He became the father of 22 sons and 16 daughters.

**22** The other actions of Abijah, his ways, and his words are in the records of the prophet Iddo.

## Chapter 14

**1** Then Abijah died, and the people buried him in the City of David. Asa his son became king after him. In the time of Asa, the land had peace for 10 years.

**2** Asa did what was good and right in the sight of the LORD his God.

**3** He removed the foreign altars and the high places. He broke the sacred stones and cut down the Asherah poles.

**4** He commanded the people of Judah to worship the LORD, the God of their ancestors. He commanded them to obey the law and the commands.

**5** He removed the high places and the incense altars from all the cities of Judah. The kingdom had peace while he ruled.

**6** He built strong cities in Judah, because the land had peace. There was no war in those years, because the LORD gave him peace.

**7** Asa said to the people of Judah, "We will build these cities. We will put walls, towers, gates, and bars around them. The land is still ours, because we obeyed the LORD our God. We obeyed him, and he gave us peace on all sides." Thus they built the cities and were successful.

**8** Asa had an army of 300,000 men from Judah with large shields and spears. He also had 280,000 men from Benjamin with small shields and bows. All these men were brave soldiers.

**9** Zerah the Ethiopian came out against them with an army of 1,000,000 men and 300 chariots. He came to Mareshah.

**10** Asa went out to fight against him. They prepared for battle in the Valley of Zephathah at Mareshah.

**11** Asa called to the LORD his God. He said, "LORD, only you can help the weak against the strong. Help us, LORD our God, because we trust you. In your name we go against this large army. LORD, you are our God. Do not let man win against you."

**12** Then the LORD defeated the Ethiopians in front of Asa and Judah. The Ethiopians ran away.

**13** Asa and his army chased them to Gerar. So many Ethiopians died that the army could not recover. The LORD and his army destroyed them. The men of Judah took very much plunder.

**14** They destroyed all the cities near Gerar, because the fear of the LORD came on these cities. They took plunder from all the cities, because there was very much plunder in them.

**15** They also attacked the camps of the herdsmen. They took many sheep and camels. Then they went back to Jerusalem.

## Chapter 15

**1** The Spirit of God came on Azariah, the son of Oded.

**2** Azariah went out to meet Asa. He said to him, "Asa, and all Judah and Benjamin, listen to me. The LORD is with you while you are with him. If you look for him, you will find him. But if you leave him, he will leave you.

**3** For a long time Israel was without the true God. They had no priest to teach them, and they had no law.

**4** But in their trouble they turned to the LORD, the God of Israel. They looked for him, and they found him.

**5** In those times nobody could travel safely. There was much trouble for all the people of the lands.

**6** One nation destroyed another nation, and one city destroyed another city. God caused them to have all types of trouble.

**7** But you must be strong. Do not stop your work, because God will reward your work."

**8** Asa heard these words, the prophecy of Azariah, the son of Oded the prophet. Then he became brave. He removed the disgusting idols from all the land of Judah and Benjamin. He also removed them from the cities that he took in the hill country of Ephraim. He repaired the altar of the LORD in front of the porch of the house of the LORD.

**9** He collected all the people of Judah and Benjamin. He also collected the people from Ephraim, Manasseh, and Simeon who lived with them. Many people from Israel came to Asa, because they saw that the LORD his God was with him.

**10** They came together in Jerusalem in the third month of the fifteenth year of the rule of Asa.

**11** On that day they offered to the LORD 700 oxen and 7,000 sheep from the plunder that they brought.

**12** They made a covenant to obey the LORD, the God of their ancestors, with all their heart and with all their soul.

**13** They agreed to kill each person who did not obey the LORD, the God of Israel. They agreed to kill young or old, man or woman.

**14** They made an oath to the LORD with a loud voice. They shouted and blew trumpets and horns.

**15** All Judah was happy about the oath, because they made the oath with all their heart. They looked for the LORD with all their desire, and they found him. The LORD gave them peace on all sides.

**16** King Asa also removed Maachah, his grandmother, from her position as queen mother. He did this because she made a disgusting Asherah idol. Asa cut down her idol, broke it into pieces, and burned it at the Kidron Valley.

**17** Asa did not remove the high places from Israel. But the heart of Asa was completely faithful to the LORD all his life.

**18** He brought into the house of God the silver, the gold, and the items that he and his father gave to God.

**19** There was no more war until the thirty-fifth year of the rule of Asa.

## Chapter 16

**1** In the thirty-sixth year of the rule of Asa, Baasha, the king of Israel, attacked Judah. He built a fort at Ramah. He did this to stop all people from going to Asa, the king of Judah, or from coming out from him.

**2** Then Asa took silver and gold from the treasuries of the house of the LORD and of the palace of the king. He sent it to Ben-hadad, the king of Syria, who lived in Damascus. Asa said,

**3** "We have a treaty, as my father and your father had. Look, I send you silver and gold. Break your treaty with Baasha, the king of Israel. Then he will go away from me."

**4** Ben-hadad agreed with King Asa. He sent the commanders of his armies against the cities of Israel. They attacked Ijon, Dan, Abel-maim, and all the store cities of Naphtali.

**5** Baasha heard about this. Then he stopped his work on the fort at Ramah.

**6** Then King Asa took all the men of Judah. They took away the stones and the wood that Baasha used at Ramah. With these materials Asa built Geba and Mizpah.

**7** At that time Hanani the seer came to Asa, the king of Judah. Hanani said to him, "You trusted the king of Syria. You did not trust the LORD your God. Thus the army of the king of Syria escaped from you.

**8** The Ethiopians and the Libyans had a very large army with very many chariots and horsemen. But you trusted the LORD, and he gave them into your power.

**9** The LORD watches all the earth. He gives strength to those whose hearts are completely faithful to him. In this matter you were foolish. Thus from now on you will have wars."

**10** Asa was angry with the seer and put him in prison. Asa was very angry with him because of these words. At the same time Asa treated some of the people cruelly.

**11** The actions of Asa, from the start to the end, are in the book of the kings of Judah and Israel.

**12** In the thirty-ninth year of his rule, Asa got a disease in his feet. The disease became very bad. But even in his disease he did not ask the LORD for help. He only asked the doctors for help.

**13** Then Asa died in the forty-first year of his rule.

**14** The people buried him in the tomb that he made for himself in the City of David. They put him on a bed full of spices and different perfumes. Skilled perfume makers prepared these spices. The people made a very large fire to honor him.

## Chapter 17

**1** Jehoshaphat, the son of Asa, became king after him. He made his kingdom strong against Israel.

**2** He put soldiers in all the strong cities of Judah. He put groups of soldiers in the land of Judah and in the cities of Ephraim that Asa his father took.

**3** The LORD was with Jehoshaphat, because he lived as his ancestor David lived in his first years. Jehoshaphat did not worship the Baals.

**4** He worshiped the God of his father and obeyed the commands of God. He did not do the things that the kings of Israel did.

**5** Thus the LORD made the kingdom strong under the control of Jehoshaphat. All the people of Judah brought gifts to Jehoshaphat. He had much riches and honor.

**6** His heart was brave to obey the LORD. He also removed the high places and the Asherah poles from Judah.

**7** In the third year of his rule, he sent his officials to teach in the cities of Judah. These officials were Ben-hail, Obadiah, Zechariah, Nethaneel, and Michaiah.

**8** He sent some Levites with them: Shemaiah, Nethaniah, Zebadiah, Asahel, Shemiramoth, Jehonathan, Adonijah, Tobijah, and Tob-adonijah. He also sent the priests Elishama and Jehoram with them.

**9** They taught in Judah. They had the book of the law of the LORD with them. They went to all the cities of Judah and taught the people.

**10** The fear of the LORD came on all the kingdoms of the lands around Judah. Thus they did not make war against Jehoshaphat.

**11** Some Philistines brought gifts and silver as tax to Jehoshaphat. The Arabs brought him flocks: 7,700 rams and 7,700 male goats.

**12** Jehoshaphat became more and more powerful. He built forts and store cities in Judah.

**13** He had many supplies in the cities of Judah. He also had brave soldiers in Jerusalem.

**14** This is the list of the soldiers by their families. From Judah, the commanders of groups of 1,000 were these: Adnah was the chief commander, with 300,000 brave soldiers.

**15** Next to him was Jehohanan the commander, with 280,000 soldiers.

**16** Next to him was Amasiah, the son of Zichri, with 200,000 brave soldiers. Amasiah gave himself willingly to serve the LORD.

**17** From Benjamin, there was Eliada, a brave soldier. With him were 200,000 soldiers with bows and shields.

**18** Next to him was Jehozabad, with 180,000 soldiers ready for war.

**19** These men served the king. The king also had soldiers in the strong cities in all Judah.

## Chapter 18

**1** Jehoshaphat had much riches and honor. He made an alliance with Ahab by marriage.

**2** After some years, Jehoshaphat went down to visit Ahab in Samaria. Ahab killed many sheep and oxen for him and for the people with him. Ahab persuaded Jehoshaphat to attack Ramoth-gilead with him.

**3** Ahab, the king of Israel, said to Jehoshaphat, the king of Judah, "Will you go with me to attack Ramoth-gilead?" Jehoshaphat answered him, "I am the same as you, and my people are the same as your people. We will go with you to the war."

**4** Jehoshaphat also said to the king of Israel, "But first, ask the LORD for advice."

**5** Thus the king of Israel collected 400 prophets. He said to them, "Must we go to fight at Ramoth-gilead, or must I not go?" They said, "Go up, because God will give the city into the power of the king."

**6** But Jehoshaphat said, "Is there another prophet of the LORD here? We can ask him."

**7** The king of Israel said to Jehoshaphat, "There is one more man. Through him we can ask the LORD. But I hate him, because he never says good things about me. He always says bad things. His name is Micaiah, the son of Imla." Jehoshaphat said, "The king must not say that."

**8** Then the king of Israel called one of his officials. He said, "Quickly bring Micaiah, the son of Imla."

**9** The king of Israel and Jehoshaphat, the king of Judah, wore their royal robes. They sat on their thrones in an open area near the entrance of the gate of Samaria. All the prophets spoke prophecies in front of them.

**10** Zedekiah, the son of Chenaanah, made horns of iron. He said, "The LORD says this: 'With these horns you will push the Syrians until you destroy them.'"

**11** All the prophets gave the same prophecy. They said, "Attack Ramoth-gilead and win, because the LORD will give it into the power of the king."

**12** The messenger who went to call Micaiah said to him, "Look, all the prophets agree. They say good things to the king. Please make your words the same as theirs. Say good things."

**13** But Micaiah said, "The LORD is alive. As surely as he lives, I will say what my God tells me."

**14** Micaiah came to the king. The king said to him, "Micaiah, must we go to fight at Ramoth-gilead, or must I not go?" Micaiah said, "Go up and win. The enemy will be given into your power."

**15** The king said to him, "How many times must I tell you to make an oath? Tell me only the truth in the name of the LORD."

**16** Then Micaiah said, "I saw all Israel spread out on the mountains, as sheep without a shepherd. The LORD said, 'These people have no master. Each man must go home in peace.'"

**17** The king of Israel said to Jehoshaphat, "I told you that he will not say good things about me. He only says bad things."

**18** Micaiah said, "Thus, hear the word of the LORD. I saw the LORD on his throne. All the army of heaven stood on his right side and on his left side.

**19** The LORD said, 'Who will trick Ahab, the king of Israel, so that he attacks Ramoth-gilead and dies there?' One said one thing, and another said a different thing.

**20** Then a spirit came forward and stood in front of the LORD. The spirit said, 'I will trick him.' The LORD said to him, 'How?'

**21** The spirit said, 'I will go out and cause all his prophets to tell lies.' The LORD said, 'You will trick him, and you will be successful. Go and do it.'

**22** Now, look, the LORD put a lying spirit in the mouths of these prophets of yours. The LORD said that disaster will come on you."

**23** Then Zedekiah, the son of Chenaanah, came near and hit Micaiah on the face. Zedekiah said, "How did the Spirit of the LORD go from me to speak to you?"

**24** Micaiah said, "You will know on the day when you go into an inner room to hide."

**25** Then the king of Israel said, "Take Micaiah. Send him back to Amon, the governor of the city, and to Joash, the son of the king.

**26** Tell them, 'The king says this: Put this man in prison. Give him only a small quantity of bread and water until I come back safely.'"

**27** Micaiah said, "If you come back safely, then the LORD did not speak through me." He also said, "Listen, all you people!"

**28** Then the king of Israel and Jehoshaphat, the king of Judah, went to attack Ramoth-gilead.

**29** The king of Israel said to Jehoshaphat, "I will change my clothes so that people do not know me. Then I will go into the battle. But you wear your royal robes." Thus the king of Israel changed his clothes, and they went into the battle.

**30** The king of Syria gave a command to the commanders of his chariots. He said, "Do not fight with any soldier, small or great. Fight only with the king of Israel."

**31** The commanders of the chariots saw Jehoshaphat. They said, "That is the king of Israel." Thus they turned to attack him. But Jehoshaphat cried out, and the LORD helped him. God caused them to go away from him.

**32** The commanders of the chariots saw that he was not the king of Israel. Then they stopped their chase of him.

**33** But a man shot an arrow at random. The arrow hit the king of Israel between the parts of his armor. The king said to his chariot driver, "Turn around and take me out of the battle. I am wounded."

**34** The battle became worse all that day. The king of Israel stayed up in his chariot and faced the Syrians until the evening. At sunset he died.

## Chapter 19

**1** Jehoshaphat, the king of Judah, came back safely to his palace in Jerusalem.

**2** Jehu, the son of Hanani the seer, went out to meet him. Jehu said to King Jehoshaphat, "You must not help evil people. You must not love those who hate the LORD. Because of this, the anger of the LORD is on you.

**3** But there are some good things in you. You removed the Asherah poles from the land, and you decided in your heart to obey God."

**4** Jehoshaphat lived in Jerusalem. He went out again among the people, from Beersheba to the hill country of Ephraim. He brought them back to the LORD, the God of their ancestors.

**5** He put judges in the land, in each of the strong cities of Judah.

**6** He said to the judges, "Be careful about what you do. You do not judge for people, but for the LORD. He is with you when you make a decision.

**7** Thus, fear the LORD. Be careful in what you do. The LORD our God does not do wrong. He does not show favor to some people, and he does not take bribes."

**8** In Jerusalem, Jehoshaphat also chose some Levites, priests, and heads of the families of Israel. They had to judge for the LORD and decide disputes. They lived in Jerusalem.

**9** He commanded them, "You must do this work with fear of the LORD. Do it faithfully and with all your heart.

**10** Your relatives who live in their cities will bring cases to you. The cases can be about murder or about the law, commands, rules, or decisions. In each case you must warn them not to sin against the LORD. If you do not warn them, the anger of the LORD will come on you and on your relatives. Do this, and you will not be guilty.

**11** Look, Amariah, the chief priest, will be over you in all matters of the LORD. Zebadiah, the son of Ishmael, the leader of the tribe of Judah, will be over you in all matters of the king. The Levites will serve as officials for you. Be brave and do your work. The LORD will be with those who do good."

## Chapter 20

**1** After this, the Moabites and the Ammonites, and some Meunites with them, came to fight against Jehoshaphat.

**2** Some men came and told Jehoshaphat, "A very large army comes against you from Edom, from the other side of the Dead Sea. Look, they are now in Hazazon-tamar, which is En-gedi."

**3** Jehoshaphat was afraid. He decided to ask the LORD for help. He told all the people of Judah to fast.

**4** The people of Judah came together to ask the LORD for help. They came from all the cities of Judah to pray to the LORD.

**5** Jehoshaphat stood in the assembly of Judah and Jerusalem at the house of the LORD, in front of the new court.

**6** He said, "LORD, God of our ancestors, you are the God in heaven. You rule over all the kingdoms of the nations. You have power and strength. Nobody can fight against you.

**7** You are our God. You pushed out the people who lived in this land, in front of your people Israel. You gave this land forever to the descendants of Abraham, your friend.

**8** They lived in this land and built a holy house in it for your name. They said,

**9** 'Disaster can come on us, such as war, punishment, disease, or famine. Then we will stand in front of this house and in front of you, because your name is in this house. We will call to you in our trouble. Then you will hear us and save us.'

**10** Now look, here are the people of Ammon, Moab, and Mount Seir. When Israel came out of Egypt, you did not let Israel attack them. Israel went around them and did not destroy them.

**11** Look how they now reward us! They come to push us out of your land, which you gave us as our possession.

**12** Our God, will you not judge them? We do not have the strength to fight this large army that comes against us. We do not know what to do, but we look to you."

**13** All the men of Judah stood in front of the LORD, with their babies, their wives, and their children.

**14** Then the Spirit of the LORD came on Jahaziel in the center of the assembly. Jahaziel was the son of Zechariah, the son of Benaiah, the son of Jeiel, the son of Mattaniah. He was a Levite and a descendant of Asaph.

**15** Jahaziel said, "Listen, all Judah, people of Jerusalem, and King Jehoshaphat. The LORD says this to you: 'Do not be afraid. Do not lose courage because of this large army. The battle is not yours, but God's.

**16** Tomorrow, go down against them. Look, they will come up by the Pass of Ziz. You will find them at the end of the valley, in front of the Wilderness of Jeruel.

**17** You will not have to fight in this battle. Take your positions, stand still, and see how the LORD saves you, Judah and Jerusalem. Do not be afraid. Do not lose courage. Tomorrow, go out against them, because the LORD will be with you.'"

**18** Jehoshaphat bowed with his face to the ground. All the people of Judah and Jerusalem went down in front of the LORD and worshiped him.

**19** Then some Levites from the Kohathites and the Korahites stood up. They praised the LORD, the God of Israel, with a very loud voice.

**20** Early in the morning, they went out into the Wilderness of Tekoa. When they went out, Jehoshaphat stood and said, "Listen to me, Judah and people of Jerusalem! Trust the LORD your God, and you will be safe. Trust his prophets, and you will be successful."

**21** Jehoshaphat talked with the people. Then he chose men to sing to the LORD and to praise him for his holy beauty. The singers went in front of the army. They sang, "Thank the LORD, because his love continues forever."

**22** When they started to sing and praise, the LORD put an ambush against the people of Ammon, Moab, and Mount Seir. These people came to attack Judah, and they were defeated.

**23** The people of Ammon and Moab attacked the people of Mount Seir. They killed them and destroyed them completely. After they destroyed the people of Seir, they attacked and destroyed each other.

**24** The men of Judah came to the watchtower in the wilderness. They looked at the large army. They saw only dead bodies on the ground. Nobody escaped.

**25** Jehoshaphat and his people came to take the plunder. They found among the dead bodies many valuable possessions, clothes, and jewels. They took more than they could carry. There was so much plunder that they needed 3 days to collect it.

**26** On the fourth day they came together in the Valley of Berachah. There they praised the LORD. Thus that place has the name Valley of Berachah, which means "praise", to this day.

**27** Then all the men of Judah and Jerusalem went back to Jerusalem with joy. Jehoshaphat went in front of them. The LORD made them happy because he defeated their enemies.

**28** They came to Jerusalem and went to the house of the LORD with lyres, harps, and trumpets.

**29** The fear of God came on all the kingdoms of those lands. They heard that the LORD fought against the enemies of Israel.

**30** Thus the kingdom of Jehoshaphat had peace, because his God gave him peace on all sides.

**31** Jehoshaphat ruled over Judah. He was 35 years old when he became king. He ruled for 25 years in Jerusalem. The name of his mother was Azubah, the daughter of Shilhi.

**32** He lived in the way of Asa his father and did not turn away from it. He did what was right in the sight of the LORD.

**33** But the people did not remove the high places. The people still did not decide in their hearts to obey the God of their ancestors.

**34** The other actions of Jehoshaphat, from the start to the end, are in the records of Jehu, the son of Hanani. These records are in the book of the kings of Israel.

**35** After this, Jehoshaphat, the king of Judah, made an alliance with Ahaziah, the king of Israel. Ahaziah did very evil things.

**36** Jehoshaphat agreed with Ahaziah to build ships to go to Tarshish. They built the ships in Ezion-geber.

**37** Then Eliezer, the son of Dodavah from Mareshah, spoke a prophecy against Jehoshaphat. He said, "You made an alliance with Ahaziah. Thus the LORD will destroy the things that you made." The ships were wrecked and could not go to Tarshish.

## Chapter 21

**1** Then Jehoshaphat died, and the people buried him with his ancestors in the City of David. Jehoram his son became king after him.

**2** Jehoram had brothers, the sons of Jehoshaphat. They were Azariah, Jehiel, Zechariah, Azariah, Michael, and Shephatiah. All these were the sons of Jehoshaphat, the king of Israel.

**3** Their father gave them many gifts of silver, gold, and valuable things. He also gave them strong cities in Judah. But he gave the kingdom to Jehoram, because Jehoram was the oldest son.

**4** Jehoram took control of the kingdom of his father and made himself strong. Then he killed all his brothers with the sword. He also killed some of the leaders of Israel.

**5** Jehoram was 32 years old when he became king. He ruled for 8 years in Jerusalem.

**6** He lived in the way of the kings of Israel, as the family of Ahab did. He married the daughter of Ahab. He did evil in the sight of the LORD.

**7** But the LORD did not want to destroy the family of David, because of the covenant that he made with David. He promised to give a lamp to David and to his descendants forever.

**8** While Jehoram ruled, Edom rebelled against Judah and made its own king.

**9** Thus Jehoram went to Edom with his commanders and all his chariots. The Edomites were around him and the commanders of his chariots. But he got up at night and attacked them.

**10** Edom rebelled against Judah, and it is free from Judah to this day. At the same time, Libnah also rebelled against Judah. This occurred because Jehoram left the LORD, the God of his ancestors.

**11** Jehoram also made high places in the hill country of Judah. He caused the people of Jerusalem to be unfaithful to God. He made the people of Judah turn away from God.

**12** Then a letter came to Jehoram from Elijah the prophet. The letter said, "The LORD, the God of your ancestor David, says this: 'You did not live in the ways of Jehoshaphat your father or in the ways of Asa, the king of Judah.

**13** But you lived in the way of the kings of Israel. You caused Judah and the people of Jerusalem to be unfaithful to God, as the family of Ahab did. You also killed your brothers, the family of your father. They were better than you.

**14** Thus the LORD will send a terrible disaster on your people, your sons, your wives, and all your possessions.

**15** You will have a very bad disease of your intestines. Each day the disease will become worse, until your intestines come out.'"

**16** The LORD caused the Philistines and the Arabs who lived near the Ethiopians to become angry with Jehoram.

**17** They attacked Judah and went into it. They took all the possessions in the palace of the king. They also took his sons and his wives. Only Jehoahaz, his youngest son, stayed with him.

**18** After all this, the LORD struck Jehoram with a disease of the intestines. There was no cure for the disease.

**19** After 2 years, his intestines came out because of the disease. He died in very bad pain. His people did not make a fire to honor him, as they did for his ancestors.

**20** Jehoram was 32 years old when he became king. He ruled in Jerusalem for 8 years. Nobody was sad when he died. The people buried him in the City of David, but not in the tombs of the kings.

## Chapter 22

**1** The people of Jerusalem made Ahaziah, the youngest son of Jehoram, king after him. The raiders who came to the camp with the Arabs killed all the older sons. Thus Ahaziah, the son of Jehoram, became the king of Judah.

**2** Ahaziah was 42 years old when he became king. He ruled for 1 year in Jerusalem. The name of his mother was Athaliah, the granddaughter of Omri.

**3** He also lived in the ways of the family of Ahab. His mother advised him to do evil.

**4** He did evil in the sight of the LORD, as the family of Ahab did. After the death of his father, they were his advisers. Their advice caused his destruction.

**5** He followed their advice. He went with Jehoram, the son of Ahab, the king of Israel, to fight against Hazael, the king of Syria, at Ramoth-gilead. The Syrians wounded Joram.

**6** Joram went back to Jezreel to get well from the wounds that he got at Ramah. He got these wounds when he fought with Hazael, the king of Syria. Ahaziah, the son of Jehoram, the king of Judah, went down to Jezreel to visit Jehoram, the son of Ahab, because Jehoram was sick.

**7** God caused the destruction of Ahaziah through this visit to Joram. When Ahaziah arrived, he went out with Jehoram to meet Jehu, the son of Nimshi. The LORD anointed Jehu to destroy the family of Ahab.

**8** Jehu punished the family of Ahab. He found the leaders of Judah and the sons of the brothers of Ahaziah, who served Ahaziah. Jehu killed them.

**9** Jehu looked for Ahaziah. His men caught Ahaziah, who hid in Samaria. They brought Ahaziah to Jehu and killed him. Then they buried him. They said, "He is the grandson of Jehoshaphat, who obeyed the LORD with all his heart." Then nobody in the family of Ahaziah had the power to rule the kingdom.

**10** Athaliah, the mother of Ahaziah, saw that her son was dead. Then she killed all the royal family of Judah.

**11** But Jehoshabeath, the daughter of the king, took Joash, the son of Ahaziah. She took him secretly from among the sons of the king who were killed. She put him and his nurse in a bedroom. Jehoshabeath was the daughter of King Jehoram and the wife of Jehoiada the priest. She was also the sister of Ahaziah. She hid Joash from Athaliah, and thus Athaliah did not kill him.

**12** Joash stayed hidden with them in the house of God for 6 years. During this time Athaliah ruled the land.

## Chapter 23

**1** In the seventh year, Jehoiada became brave. He made a covenant with the commanders of groups of 100 men. These commanders were Azariah, the son of Jeroham; Ishmael, the son of Jehohanan; Azariah, the son of Obed; Maaseiah, the son of Adaiah; and Elishaphat, the son of Zichri.

**2** They went through Judah. They collected the Levites from all the cities of Judah and the heads of the families of Israel. These people came to Jerusalem.

**3** All the assembly made a covenant with the king in the house of God. Jehoiada said to them, "Look, the son of the king will rule, as the LORD promised about the descendants of David.

**4** This is what you must do. You priests and Levites who come on duty on the Sabbath, divide into 3 groups. One third of you must guard the doors.

**5** One third must be at the palace of the king. One third must be at the Foundation Gate. All the people must be in the courts of the house of the LORD.

**6** Do not let anyone come into the house of the LORD except the priests and the Levites who serve. They can go in, because they are holy. All the other people must obey the command of the LORD and stay outside.

**7** The Levites must stand around the king, each man with his weapons in his hand. Kill any other person who comes into the house. Stay with the king when he comes in and when he goes out."

**8** The Levites and all Judah did all the things that Jehoiada the priest commanded. Each commander took his men who came on duty on the Sabbath and the men who went off duty on the Sabbath. Jehoiada the priest did not let any division go home.

**9** Jehoiada the priest gave the commanders of groups of 100 men the spears and the large and small shields of King David. These weapons were in the house of God.

**10** He put all the people in their positions, each man with his weapon in his hand. They stood from the right side of the temple to the left side of the temple, near the altar and the temple. They stood around the king.

**11** Then they brought out the son of the king and put the crown on him. They gave him a copy of the law and made him king. Jehoiada and his sons anointed him. They shouted, "Long live the king!"

**12** Athaliah heard the noise of the people who ran and praised the king. She went to the people at the house of the LORD.

**13** She looked and saw the king. He stood by his pillar at the entrance. The commanders and the trumpet players were next to the king. All the people of the land were happy and blew trumpets. The singers with instruments of music led the praise. Then Athaliah tore her clothes and shouted, "Treason! Treason!"

**14** Jehoiada the priest sent out the commanders of groups of 100 men who led the army. He said to them, "Take her out between the lines of soldiers. Kill with the sword any person who follows her." The priest said, "Do not kill her in the house of the LORD."

**15** Thus they took hold of her. She went to the entrance of the Horse Gate near the palace of the king. There they killed her.

**16** Then Jehoiada made a covenant between himself, all the people, and the king. They agreed to be the people of the LORD.

**17** Then all the people went to the temple of Baal and pulled it down. They broke its altars and its idols into pieces. They killed Mattan, the priest of Baal, in front of the altars.

**18** Jehoiada put the work of the house of the LORD under the control of the priests and the Levites. David divided them into groups for the house of the LORD. They had to offer the burnt offerings to the LORD, as the law of Moses tells. They had to offer them with joy and songs, as David commanded.

**19** He put gatekeepers at the gates of the house of the LORD. Thus no person who was unclean for any reason could go in.

**20** He took the commanders of groups of 100 men, the important men, the rulers of the people, and all the people of the land. They brought the king down from the house of the LORD. They went through the Upper Gate to the palace of the king. They put the king on the royal throne.

**21** All the people of the land were happy. The city had peace after they killed Athaliah with the sword.

## Chapter 24

**1** Joash was 7 years old when he became king. He ruled for 40 years in Jerusalem. The name of his mother was Zibiah from Beersheba.

**2** Joash did what was right in the sight of the LORD while Jehoiada the priest lived.

**3** Jehoiada chose 2 wives for Joash. Joash became the father of sons and daughters.

**4** After this, Joash decided to repair the house of the LORD.

**5** He called the priests and the Levites together. He said to them, "Go out to the cities of Judah. Collect money from all Israel each year to repair the house of your God. Do this quickly." But the Levites did not do it quickly.

**6** Thus the king called Jehoiada, the chief priest. The king said to him, "Why did you not make the Levites bring in the tax from Judah and from Jerusalem? Moses, the servant of the LORD, and the assembly of Israel made this tax for the tent of the covenant."

**7** The sons of Athaliah, that evil woman, broke into the house of God. They also used all the holy items of the house of the LORD for the Baals.

**8** The king gave a command, and the people made a box. They put it outside, at the gate of the house of the LORD.

**9** Then they sent a message through Judah and Jerusalem. The people had to bring to the LORD the tax that Moses, the servant of God, gave to Israel in the wilderness.

**10** All the leaders and all the people were happy. They brought their money and put it into the box until the box was full.

**11** The Levites brought the box to the officials of the king. When they saw that there was much money, the secretary of the king and the officer of the chief priest came. They emptied the box and took the box back to its place. They did this each day and collected much money.

**12** The king and Jehoiada gave the money to the men who supervised the work on the house of the LORD. These men paid stone workers and carpenters to repair the house of the LORD. They also paid men who worked with iron and bronze to repair the house of the LORD.

**13** The workers did their work, and the repairs continued well under their control. They made the house of God as it was before and made it strong.

**14** When they completed the work, they brought the remaining money to the king and Jehoiada. With the money they made items for the house of the LORD. They made items for the service and for the burnt offerings. They also made spoons and other items of gold and silver. While Jehoiada lived, the people offered burnt offerings in the house of the LORD all the time.

**15** Jehoiada became very old and died. He was 130 years old when he died.

**16** The people buried him with the kings in the City of David, because he did good things in Israel for God and for the house of God.

**17** After the death of Jehoiada, the leaders of Judah came and bowed in front of the king. Then the king listened to them.

**18** They left the house of the LORD, the God of their ancestors. They worshiped Asherah poles and idols. Because of this sin, the anger of God came on Judah and Jerusalem.

**19** But the LORD sent prophets to them to bring them back to him. The prophets warned the people, but the people did not listen.

**20** Then the Spirit of God came on Zechariah, the son of Jehoiada the priest. Zechariah stood above the people and said to them, "God says this: 'Why do you disobey the commands of the LORD? You will not be successful. You left the LORD, thus he left you.'"

**21** But the people made a plan against Zechariah. The king gave the command, and they threw stones at Zechariah until he died. They did this in the court of the house of the LORD.

**22** Thus King Joash did not remember the kindness of Jehoiada, the father of Zechariah. Joash killed the son of Jehoiada. When Zechariah died, he said, "The LORD will see this and punish you."

**23** At the end of the year, the army of Syria attacked Joash. They came to Judah and Jerusalem and killed all the leaders of the people. They sent all the plunder to the king of Damascus.

**24** The Syrian army came with only a small group of men. But the LORD gave a very large army of Judah into their power, because Judah left the LORD, the God of their ancestors. Thus the Syrians punished Joash.

**25** The Syrians went away and left Joash badly wounded. Then his own servants made a plan against him because he killed the son of Jehoiada the priest. They killed Joash on his bed, and he died. The people buried him in the City of David, but not in the tombs of the kings.

**26** These are the men who made the plan against him: Zabad, the son of Shimeath, an Ammonite woman, and Jehozabad, the son of Shimrith, a Moabite woman.

**27** The record of his sons, the many prophecies against him, and the repairs of the house of God are in the book of the kings. Amaziah his son became king after him.

## Chapter 25

**1** Amaziah was 25 years old when he became king. He ruled for 29 years in Jerusalem. The name of his mother was Jehoaddan from Jerusalem.

**2** He did what was right in the sight of the LORD, but not with all his heart.

**3** When he had firm control of the kingdom, he killed the officials who killed the king, his father.

**4** But he did not kill their children. He obeyed the law in the book of Moses. In this law the LORD commanded, "Do not kill fathers because of the sins of their children. Do not kill children because of the sins of their fathers. Each person must die for his own sin."

**5** Amaziah collected the people of Judah. He put them under commanders of groups of 1,000 and of 100, by families, in all Judah and Benjamin. He counted the men 20 years old and older. There were 300,000 chosen men who could go to war and use spear and shield.

**6** He also paid 100 talents of silver to get 100,000 brave soldiers from Israel.

**7** But a man of God came to him and said, "King, do not let the army of Israel go with you. The LORD is not with Israel, with all the people of Ephraim.

**8** If you go, and if you fight bravely in the battle, God will still let the enemy defeat you. God has the power to help or to cause defeat."

**9** Amaziah said to the man of God, "But what about the 100 talents of silver that I gave to the army of Israel?" The man of God answered, "The LORD can give you much more than this."

**10** Thus Amaziah sent the army that came to him from Ephraim back home. These soldiers became very angry with Judah and went home in great anger.

**11** Then Amaziah became brave and led his army to the Valley of Salt. There he killed 10,000 men of Seir.

**12** The army of Judah also caught 10,000 men alive. They took them to the top of a cliff and threw them down from the cliff. All the men died.

**13** But the soldiers that Amaziah sent back attacked the cities of Judah, from Samaria to Beth-horon. They killed 3,000 people and took much plunder.

**14** Amaziah came back after he killed the Edomites. He brought the gods of the people of Seir. He made them his own gods. He bowed down in front of them and burned incense to them.

**15** Thus the LORD became angry with Amaziah. He sent a prophet to him. The prophet said, "Why do you worship the gods of this people? These gods could not save their own people from you."

**16** While the prophet spoke, the king said to him, "Did we make you an adviser to the king? Stop! Why must we kill you?" The prophet stopped. But first he said, "I know that God decided to destroy you. You did this thing, and you did not listen to my advice."

**17** Then Amaziah, the king of Judah, got advice. He sent a message to Joash, the son of Jehoahaz, the son of Jehu, the king of Israel. The message said, "Come, we will meet in battle."

**18** But Joash, the king of Israel, sent this answer to Amaziah, the king of Judah: "A thistle in Lebanon sent a message to a cedar in Lebanon. The thistle said, 'Give your daughter to my son as a wife.' Then a wild animal in Lebanon went past and walked on the thistle.

**19** You say that you defeated Edom. Now you are proud and you boast. Stay at home! Why do you cause trouble for yourself? You will fall, and Judah will fall with you."

**20** But Amaziah did not listen. God caused this, because God wanted to give them into the power of their enemies. He did this because they worshiped the gods of Edom.

**21** Thus Joash, the king of Israel, attacked. He and Amaziah, the king of Judah, met in battle at Beth-shemesh in Judah.

**22** Israel defeated Judah, and each man of Judah ran away to his home.

**23** Joash, the king of Israel, caught Amaziah, the king of Judah, at Beth-shemesh. Amaziah was the son of Joash, the son of Jehoahaz. Joash brought him to Jerusalem. He broke down the wall of Jerusalem from the Ephraim Gate to the Corner Gate. This part of the wall was 400 cubits long.

**24** He took all the gold, the silver, and all the items in the house of God, which Obed-edom kept. He also took the treasures of the palace of the king and some hostages. Then he went back to Samaria.

**25** Amaziah, the son of Joash, the king of Judah, lived 15 years after the death of Joash, the son of Jehoahaz, the king of Israel.

**26** The other actions of Amaziah, from the start to the end, are in the book of the kings of Judah and Israel.

**27** Amaziah turned away from the LORD. After that time, people in Jerusalem made a plan against him. He ran away to Lachish. But they sent men after him to Lachish, and these men killed him there.

**28** They brought his body back on horses. They buried him with his ancestors in the City of Judah.

## Chapter 26

**1** Then all the people of Judah took Uzziah, who was 16 years old. They made him king after his father Amaziah.

**2** After the death of Amaziah, Uzziah built Eloth again and gave it back to Judah.

**3** Uzziah was 16 years old when he became king. He ruled for 52 years in Jerusalem. The name of his mother was Jecoliah from Jerusalem.

**4** He did what was right in the sight of the LORD, as his father Amaziah did.

**5** He obeyed God while Zechariah lived. Zechariah taught him to fear God. While Uzziah obeyed the LORD, God made him successful.

**6** Uzziah went out and fought against the Philistines. He broke down the walls of Gath, Jabneh, and Ashdod. He built cities near Ashdod and in other areas of the Philistines.

**7** God helped him against the Philistines, against the Arabs who lived in Gur-baal, and against the Meunites.

**8** The Ammonites paid tax to Uzziah. His fame went as far as the border of Egypt, because he became very strong.

**9** Uzziah built towers in Jerusalem at the Corner Gate, at the Valley Gate, and at the corner of the wall. He made these towers strong.

**10** He also built towers in the wilderness and dug many wells, because he had many animals in the low hills and in the plains. He had farmers and vineyard workers in the hills and in the fertile land, because he loved farming.

**11** Uzziah had an army of soldiers ready for war. They went out to battle in divisions. Jeiel the secretary and Maaseiah the officer counted them. Hananiah, one of the commanders of the king, controlled them.

**12** There were 2,600 heads of families who led the brave soldiers.

**13** Under their control was an army of 307,500 men. These soldiers fought with great power and helped the king against his enemies.

**14** Uzziah supplied all the army with shields, spears, helmets, armor, bows, and slings for stones.

**15** In Jerusalem he made machines that skilled men designed. He put them on the towers and on the corners of the walls. These machines could shoot arrows and large stones. His fame went far, because God helped him very much until he became strong.

**16** But when Uzziah became strong, he became proud, and this caused his destruction. He was not faithful to the LORD his God. He went into the temple of the LORD to burn incense on the altar of incense.

**17** Azariah the priest went in after him with 80 brave priests of the LORD.

**18** They stood against King Uzziah. They said to him, "Uzziah, you must not burn incense to the LORD. Only the priests, the descendants of Aaron, can burn incense. God made them holy for this work. Go out of the holy place, because you were not faithful. The LORD God will not honor you for this."

**19** Uzziah had a fire pan in his hand to burn incense, and he became angry. While he was angry with the priests, leprosy appeared on his forehead. This occurred in front of the priests in the house of the LORD, next to the incense altar.

**20** Azariah the chief priest and all the priests looked at him. They saw leprosy on his forehead. They quickly pushed him out. Uzziah also wanted to go out quickly, because the LORD struck him with the disease.

**21** King Uzziah had leprosy until the day of his death. He lived in a separate house because of the leprosy. He could not go into the house of the LORD. Jotham his son controlled the palace of the king and ruled the people of the land.

**22** Isaiah the prophet, the son of Amoz, wrote the other actions of Uzziah, from the start to the end.

**23** Then Uzziah died. The people buried him near his ancestors in a field for the burials of the kings. They did this because they said, "He has leprosy." Jotham his son became king after him.

## Chapter 27

**1** Jotham was 25 years old when he became king. He ruled for 16 years in Jerusalem. The name of his mother was Jerushah, the daughter of Zadok.

**2** He did what was right in the sight of the LORD, as his father Uzziah did. But Jotham did not go into the temple of the LORD. The people still did evil things.

**3** Jotham built the Upper Gate of the house of the LORD. He did much work on the wall at Ophel.

**4** He built cities in the hill country of Judah. He built forts and towers in the forests.

**5** He fought against the king of the Ammonites and defeated them. That year the Ammonites gave him 100 talents of silver, 10,000 measures of wheat, and 10,000 measures of barley. The Ammonites paid him the same quantity in the second year and in the third year.

**6** Thus Jotham became powerful, because he lived carefully in obedience to the LORD his God.

**7** The other actions of Jotham, all his wars, and his ways are in the book of the kings of Israel and Judah.

**8** He was 25 years old when he became king. He ruled for 16 years in Jerusalem.

**9** Then Jotham died, and the people buried him in the City of David. Ahaz his son became king after him.

## Chapter 28

**1** Ahaz was 20 years old when he became king. He ruled for 16 years in Jerusalem. He did not do what was right in the sight of the LORD, as his ancestor David did.

**2** He lived in the ways of the kings of Israel. He also made metal idols of the Baals.

**3** He burned incense in the Valley of the Son of Hinnom. He burned his sons as sacrifices in the fire. He did the disgusting things of the nations that the LORD pushed out in front of the people of Israel.

**4** He offered sacrifices and burned incense at the high places, on the hills, and under each green tree.

**5** Thus the LORD his God gave him into the power of the king of Syria. The Syrians defeated him. They took many of his people as prisoners and brought them to Damascus. God also gave Ahaz into the power of the king of Israel, who killed very many of his people.

**6** Pekah, the son of Remaliah, killed 120,000 soldiers in Judah in one day. All of them were brave men. This occurred because they left the LORD, the God of their ancestors.

**7** Zichri, a strong soldier from Ephraim, killed Maaseiah, the son of the king. He also killed Azrikam, the official in charge of the palace, and Elkanah, the second in command to the king.

**8** The Israelites took 200,000 of their relatives as prisoners: women, sons, and daughters. They also took much plunder and brought it to Samaria.

They took them to their relatives at Jericho, the City of Palm Trees. Then the men went back to Samaria.

**16** At that time King Ahaz asked the kings of Assyria for help.

**18** The Philistines also attacked the cities in the low hills and in the south of Judah. They took Beth-shemesh, Ajalon, Gederoth, Shocho, Timnah, and Gimzo, with their villages. The Philistines lived there.

**19** The LORD made Judah weak because of Ahaz, the king of Israel. Ahaz caused Judah to do evil and was very unfaithful to the LORD.

**20** Tilgath-pilneser, the king of Assyria, came to Ahaz. But he caused trouble for Ahaz. He did not help him.

**21** Ahaz took some valuable things from the house of the LORD, from the palace of the king, and from the leaders. He gave them to the king of Assyria, but that did not help him.

**22** In his time of trouble, King Ahaz became even more unfaithful to the LORD.

**23** He offered sacrifices to the gods of Damascus, the people who defeated him. He said, "The gods of the kings of Syria help them. Thus I will sacrifice to these gods, so that they help me." But these gods caused the destruction of Ahaz and of all Israel.

## Chapter 29

**1** Hezekiah was 25 years old when he became king. He ruled for 29 years in Jerusalem. The name of his mother was Abijah, the daughter of Zechariah.

**2** He did what was right in the sight of the LORD, as his ancestor David did.

**3** In the first month of the first year of his rule, he opened the doors of the house of the LORD and repaired them.

**4** He brought in the priests and the Levites. He collected them in the open area on the east side.

**5** He said to them, "Levites, listen to me! Make yourselves holy now. Make the house of the LORD, the God of your ancestors, holy. Take all the unclean things out of the holy place.

**6** Our fathers were not faithful. They did evil in the sight of the LORD our God and left him. They turned their faces away from the house of the LORD and turned their backs on it.

**7** They also shut the doors of the porch and put out the lamps. They did not burn incense or offer burnt offerings to the God of Israel in the holy place.

**8** Thus the anger of the LORD came on Judah and Jerusalem. He made them an example of terror, shock, and scorn. You can see this with your own eyes.

**9** Because of this, our fathers died in battle. Our sons, our daughters, and our wives are prisoners.

**10** Now I want to make a covenant with the LORD, the God of Israel. Then his terrible anger will turn away from us.

**11** My sons, do not be careless now. The LORD chose you to stand in front of him and serve him. You must serve him and burn incense to him."

**12** Then these Levites started to work. From the Kohathites: Mahath, the son of Amasai, and Joel, the son of Azariah. From the Merarites: Kish, the son of Abdi, and Azariah, the son of Jehalelel. From the Gershonites: Joah, the son of Zimmah, and Eden, the son of Joah.

**13** From the descendants of Elizaphan: Shimri and Jeiel. From the descendants of Asaph: Zechariah and Mattaniah.

**14** From the descendants of Heman: Jehiel and Shimei. From the descendants of Jeduthun: Shemaiah and Uzziel.

**16** The priests went into the inner part of the house of the LORD to make it clean. They brought out all the unclean things that they found in the temple of the LORD into the court of the house of the LORD. The Levites took these things out to the Kidron Valley.

**17** They started to make the house holy on the first day of the first month. On the eighth day of the month they came to the porch of the LORD. Then they made the house of the LORD holy in 8 more days. They completed the work on the sixteenth day of the first month.

**18** Then they went to King Hezekiah and said, "We made all the house of the LORD clean. We made the altar of burnt offering and all its tools clean. We made the table for the holy bread and all its items clean.

**21** They brought 7 bulls, 7 rams, 7 lambs, and 7 male goats as a sin offering for the kingdom, for the holy place, and for Judah. The king commanded the priests, the descendants of Aaron, to offer them on the altar of the LORD.

**22** Thus the priests killed the bulls, took the blood, and put it on the altar. Then they killed the rams and put the blood on the altar. They also killed the lambs and put the blood on the altar.

**23** Then they brought the male goats for the sin offering to the king and the assembly. The king and the assembly put their hands on the goats.

**24** The priests killed the goats and put their blood on the altar as a sin offering. They did this to remove the sin of all Israel. The king commanded that the burnt offering and the sin offering were for all Israel.

**25** Hezekiah put the Levites in the house of the LORD with cymbals, lyres, and harps. He did this as David, Gad the seer of the king, and Nathan the prophet commanded. The LORD gave this command through his prophets.

**26** The Levites stood with the instruments of David, and the priests stood with the trumpets.

**27** Hezekiah commanded them to offer the burnt offering on the altar. When the burnt offering started, the song to the LORD also started. The trumpets and the instruments of David, the king of Israel, played with the song.

**29** When they completed the offerings, the king and all the people with him bowed down and worshiped.

**35** There were many burnt offerings, with the fat of the peace offerings and the drink offerings for each burnt offering. Thus the service of the house of the LORD started again.

**36** Hezekiah and all the people were happy because of what God did for the people. All this occurred very quickly.

**1** Hezekiah sent messages to all Israel and Judah. He also wrote letters to Ephraim and Manasseh. He told them to come to the house of the LORD in Jerusalem to hold the Passover for the LORD, the God of Israel.

**2** The king, his leaders, and all the assembly in Jerusalem decided to hold the Passover in the second month.

**3** They could not hold it at the usual time. Not sufficient priests made themselves holy, and the people did not come together in Jerusalem.

**4** The plan was good in the opinion of the king and all the assembly.

**5** Thus they decided to send a message through all Israel, from Beersheba to Dan. The message told the people to come to Jerusalem and hold the Passover for the LORD, the God of Israel. For a long time, many people did not hold it as the law told.

**6** Thus messengers went through all Israel and Judah with letters from the king and his leaders. As the king commanded, they said, "People of Israel, come back to the LORD, the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Israel. Then he will come back to you who escaped from the kings of Assyria.

**7** Do not be like your fathers and your relatives. They were unfaithful to the LORD, the God of their ancestors. Thus he made them an example of destruction, as you can see.

**8** Now do not be stubborn, as your fathers were. Obey the LORD. Come to his holy place, which he made holy forever. Serve the LORD your God. Then his terrible anger will turn away from you.

**10** The messengers went from city to city through the land of Ephraim and Manasseh, as far as Zebulun. But the people laughed at them and made fun of them.

**15** Then they killed the Passover lambs on the fourteenth day of the second month. The priests and the Levites were ashamed, and they made themselves holy. They brought burnt offerings to the house of the LORD.

**17** Many people in the assembly did not make themselves holy. Thus the Levites killed the Passover lambs for all the people who were not clean. The Levites did this to make the lambs holy to the LORD.

**18** Many of the people from Ephraim, Manasseh, Issachar, and Zebulun did not make themselves clean. But they ate the Passover, and this was not as the law told. But Hezekiah prayed for them. He said, "The LORD is good. May he forgive each person

**19** who decides in his heart to worship God, the LORD, the God of his ancestors. Forgive him also when he is not clean as the rules of the holy place tell."

**21** The people of Israel in Jerusalem held the Feast of Unleavened Bread for 7 days with great joy. Each day the Levites and the priests praised the LORD. They sang to the LORD with loud instruments.

**22** Hezekiah spoke kind words to all the Levites who showed good skill in the service of the LORD. The people ate the food of the feast for 7 days. They offered peace offerings and thanked the LORD, the God of their ancestors.

**23** Then all the assembly decided to continue the feast for 7 more days. Thus they held the feast for 7 more days with joy.

**24** Hezekiah, the king of Judah, gave 1,000 bulls and 7,000 sheep to the assembly. The leaders gave 1,000 bulls and 10,000 sheep to the assembly. Many priests made themselves holy.

**25** All the assembly of Judah was happy. The priests and the Levites, all the people who came from Israel, and the foreigners from Israel and Judah were also happy.

**26** There was great joy in Jerusalem. Nothing like this occurred in Jerusalem since the time of Solomon, the son of David, the king of Israel.

**27** Then the priests and the Levites stood and blessed the people. God heard them. Their prayer went up to his holy home in heaven.

## Chapter 31

**1** When all this was complete, all the Israelites who were there went out to the cities of Judah. They broke the sacred stones into pieces and cut down the Asherah poles. They destroyed the high places and the altars in all Judah and Benjamin, and in Ephraim and Manasseh. They destroyed all of them. Then all the Israelites went back to their own cities and to their own land.

**2** Hezekiah put the priests and the Levites into their divisions. He gave each man his work. The priests and the Levites offered burnt offerings and peace offerings. They served, thanked, and praised God at the gates of the house of the LORD.

**3** The king gave some of his own possessions for the burnt offerings. These were the morning and evening burnt offerings and the burnt offerings for the Sabbaths, the new moons, and the feasts. The law of the LORD told about these offerings.

**4** He commanded the people who lived in Jerusalem to give the priests and the Levites their share. Then the priests and the Levites could give all their time to the law of the LORD.

**18** They gave shares to all the families in the lists, to their babies, their wives, their sons, and their daughters. They gave shares to all the assembly, because the priests and the Levites faithfully made themselves holy.

**20** Hezekiah did this in all Judah. He did what was good, right, and true in the sight of the LORD his God.

**21** He did all his work for the house of God with all his heart. He obeyed the law and the commands, and he worshiped his God. Thus he was successful.

**1** After these faithful actions of Hezekiah, Sennacherib, the king of Assyria, came and attacked Judah. He made camps around the strong cities. He wanted to take control of them.

**2** Hezekiah saw that Sennacherib came and that he wanted to attack Jerusalem.

**3** Then Hezekiah made a plan with his officials and his commanders. They decided to stop the water from the springs outside the city. The officials and the commanders helped him.

**4** Many people came together and stopped all the springs and the stream that went through the land. They said, "Why must the kings of Assyria come and find much water?"

**5** Hezekiah worked hard to make the city strong. He repaired all the broken parts of the wall and built towers on it. He built another wall outside it. He made the Millo in the City of David stronger. He also made many weapons and shields.

**6** He put commanders over the people. He brought the people together in the open area at the city gate. He spoke words of encouragement to them. He said,

**7** "Be strong and brave. Do not be afraid. Do not lose courage because of the king of Assyria or his large army. There is a greater power with us than with him.

**8** He has only human strength. But we have the LORD our God to help us and to fight our battles." The people trusted the words of Hezekiah, the king of Judah.

**9** After this, Sennacherib, the king of Assyria, sent his officials to Jerusalem. He and all his army were at Lachish and attacked it. His officials brought a message to Hezekiah, the king of Judah, and to all the people of Judah in Jerusalem. The message said,

**10** "Sennacherib, the king of Assyria, says this: 'What do you trust? Why do you stay in Jerusalem while my army surrounds it?

**11** Hezekiah says to you, "The LORD our God will save us from the king of Assyria." But Hezekiah tricks you. You will die from hunger and thirst.

**12** Hezekiah himself removed the high places and the altars of the LORD. He told Judah and Jerusalem, "You must worship at only one altar and burn incense on it."

**13** You know what I and my ancestors did to all the peoples of other lands. The gods of those nations could not save their lands from me.

**14** My ancestors destroyed those nations. None of their gods could save his people from me. Thus your God cannot save you from me!

**15** Do not let Hezekiah trick you. Do not let him persuade you in this way. Do not believe him. No god of any nation or kingdom could save his people from me or from my ancestors. Thus your God can surely not save you from me!'"

**17** Sennacherib also wrote letters to insult the LORD, the God of Israel. The letters said, "The gods of the nations of other lands did not save their people from me. Thus the God of Hezekiah will not save his people from me."

**18** The officials shouted loudly in the Hebrew language to the people of Jerusalem on the wall. They wanted to make the people afraid. Then they could take the city.

**19** They spoke about the God of Jerusalem as they spoke about the gods of the other peoples of the earth. Those gods were only things that men made with their hands.

**20** Because of this, King Hezekiah and the prophet Isaiah, the son of Amoz, prayed and cried out to heaven.

**21** Then the LORD sent an angel. The angel killed all the brave soldiers, the leaders, and the commanders in the camp of the king of Assyria. Thus Sennacherib went back to his own land in shame. He went into the temple of his god. There some of his own sons killed him with the sword.

**22** Thus the LORD saved Hezekiah and the people of Jerusalem from Sennacherib, the king of Assyria, and from all other enemies. He gave them peace on all sides.

**23** Many people brought gifts for the LORD to Jerusalem. They also brought valuable gifts to Hezekiah, the king of Judah. From then on, all the nations honored Hezekiah.

**24** At that time Hezekiah became so sick that he almost died. He prayed to the LORD. The LORD answered him and gave him a sign.

**25** But Hezekiah did not thank God for this kindness, because he became proud. Thus the anger of God came on him and on Judah and Jerusalem.

**26** Then Hezekiah and the people of Jerusalem made themselves humble because of their pride. Thus the anger of the LORD did not come on them while Hezekiah lived.

**27** Hezekiah had very much riches and honor. He made treasuries for his silver, gold, valuable stones, spices, shields, and all types of valuable items.

**28** He also made storehouses for the grain, the new wine, and the olive oil. He made stalls for all types of animals and pens for the flocks.

**29** He built cities for himself. He had very many flocks and herds, because God gave him very many possessions.

**30** Hezekiah also stopped the upper outlet of the Gihon spring. He made the water go down to the west side of the City of David. Hezekiah was successful in all his work.

**31** But the leaders of Babylon sent officials to Hezekiah. They asked about the miracle that occurred in the land. Then God left Hezekiah alone to test him. God wanted to know all the things in his heart.

**32** The other actions of Hezekiah and his good deeds are in the vision of the prophet Isaiah, the son of Amoz. They are also in the book of the kings of Judah and Israel.

**33** Then Hezekiah died. The people buried him in the highest place among the tombs of the descendants of David. All Judah and the people of Jerusalem honored him at his death. Manasseh his son became king after him.

## Chapter 33

**1** Manasseh was 12 years old when he became king. He ruled for 55 years in Jerusalem.

**2** He did evil in the sight of the LORD. He did the disgusting things of the nations that the LORD pushed out in front of the people of Israel.

**3** He built again the high places that his father Hezekiah destroyed. He built altars for the Baals and made Asherah poles. He worshiped and served all the stars in the sky.

**4** He built altars in the house of the LORD. The LORD said about this house, "My name will be in Jerusalem forever."

**5** He built altars for all the stars in the sky in the 2 courts of the house of the LORD.

**10** The LORD spoke to Manasseh and to his people, but they did not listen.

**11** Thus the LORD brought the commanders of the army of the king of Assyria against them. They caught Manasseh with hooks and put bronze chains on him. They took him to Babylon.

**12** In his trouble, Manasseh asked the LORD his God for help. He made himself very humble in front of the God of his ancestors.

**13** He prayed to God, and God listened to him. God heard his request and brought him back to Jerusalem and to his kingdom. Then Manasseh knew that the LORD is God.

**14** After this, Manasseh built an outer wall for the City of David. The wall was on the west side of the Gihon spring, in the valley. It went as far as the entrance of the Fish Gate and went around Ophel. He made the wall very high. He put army commanders in all the strong cities of Judah.

**15** He removed the foreign gods and the idol from the house of the LORD. He removed all the altars that he built on the hill of the house of the LORD and in Jerusalem. He threw them out of the city.

**16** He repaired the altar of the LORD. He offered peace offerings and thank offerings on it. He commanded Judah to serve the LORD, the God of Israel.

**17** But the people still offered sacrifices at the high places, but only to the LORD their God.

**18** The other actions of Manasseh, his prayer to his God, and the words of the seers who spoke to him in the name of the LORD, the God of Israel, are in the records of the kings of Israel.

**19** His prayer and how God heard him are in the records of the seers. All his sins and his unfaithfulness are also there. These records also tell about the places where he built high places and put up Asherah poles and idols before he made himself humble.

**20** Then Manasseh died, and the people buried him in his palace. Amon his son became king after him.

**21** Amon was 22 years old when he became king. He ruled for 2 years in Jerusalem.

**22** He did evil in the sight of the LORD, as his father Manasseh did. Amon offered sacrifices to all the idols that his father Manasseh made, and he served them.

**23** But Amon did not make himself humble in front of the LORD, as his father Manasseh did. Amon sinned more and more.

**24** His officials made a plan against him and killed him in his palace.

**25** But the people of the land killed all the men who made the plan against King Amon. Then the people of the land made Josiah, his son, king after him.

## Chapter 34

**1** Josiah was 8 years old when he became king. He ruled for 31 years in Jerusalem.

**2** He did what was right in the sight of the LORD. He lived in the ways of his ancestor David. He did not turn away to the right or to the left.

**3** In the eighth year of his rule, while he was still young, he started to worship the God of his ancestor David. In the twelfth year, he started to make Judah and Jerusalem clean. He removed the high places, the Asherah poles, the carved idols, and the metal idols.

**4** In his presence the people broke down the altars of the Baals. He cut down the incense altars above them. He broke the Asherah poles, the carved idols, and the metal idols into pieces. He crushed them into dust. He threw the dust on the graves of the people who offered sacrifices to them.

**5** He burned the bones of the priests on their altars. Thus he made Judah and Jerusalem clean.

**6** He did the same in the cities of Manasseh, Ephraim, and Simeon, as far as Naphtali, and in the ruins around them.

**7** He broke down the altars and the Asherah poles. He crushed the idols into powder. He cut down all the incense altars in all the land of Israel. Then he went back to Jerusalem.

**8** In the eighteenth year of his rule, Josiah made the land and the house of the LORD clean. Then he sent Shaphan, the son of Azaliah, Maaseiah, the governor of the city, and Joah, the son of Joahaz, the record keeper. He sent them to repair the house of the LORD his God.

**9** They came to Hilkiah, the high priest. They gave him the money that the people brought into the house of God. The Levites who guarded the doors collected this money. They collected it from the people of Manasseh, Ephraim, and all the rest of Israel, and from all Judah and Benjamin, and from the people of Jerusalem.

**10** They gave the money to the men who supervised the work on the house of the LORD. These men paid the workers who repaired the house of the LORD.

**11** They gave money to the carpenters and the builders. These workers bought cut stone and wood for beams. They used the wood for the buildings that the kings of Judah let fall into ruin.

**12** The men did the work faithfully. Their supervisors were Jahath and Obadiah, Levites from the descendants of Merari. Zechariah and Meshullam, from the descendants of Kohath, also supervised the work. Other Levites who had skill with instruments of music also helped.

**13** These Levites supervised the men who carried loads. They supervised all the workers in all their different work. Some Levites were secretaries, officials, and gatekeepers.

**14** The men brought out the money that people brought into the house of the LORD. At that time Hilkiah the priest found the book of the law of the LORD that God gave through Moses.

**15** Hilkiah said to Shaphan the secretary, "I found the book of the law in the house of the LORD." Hilkiah gave the book to Shaphan.

**16** Shaphan took the book to the king. He also gave the king a report. He said, "Your officials do all the things that you told them to do.

**17** They emptied out the money that was in the house of the LORD. They gave it to the supervisors and to the workers."

**18** Then Shaphan the secretary told the king, "Hilkiah the priest gave me a book." Shaphan read from the book to the king.

**19** The king heard the words of the law. Then he tore his clothes.

**20** The king gave a command to Hilkiah, Ahikam, the son of Shaphan, Abdon, the son of Micah, Shaphan the secretary, and Asaiah, the servant of the king. He said,

**21** "Go and ask the LORD for me and for the people who remain in Israel and in Judah. Ask about the words of this book that Hilkiah found. The LORD is very angry with us. Our ancestors did not obey the word of the LORD. They did not do all the things that this book tells."

**22** Thus Hilkiah and the men that the king sent went to Huldah the prophetess. She was the wife of Shallum, the son of Tikvath, the son of Hasrah. Shallum was in charge of the clothes. Huldah lived in the Second District of Jerusalem. They told her what occurred.

**23** She said to them, "The LORD, the God of Israel, says this: 'Tell the man who sent you to me,

**24** "The LORD says this: Look, I will bring disaster on this place and on its people. I will bring all the curses that are in the book. The people read this book to the king of Judah.

**25** The people left me. They burned incense to other gods. They made me angry with all the idols that they made with their hands. Thus my anger will come on this place, and nothing will stop it."'

**26** But tell the king of Judah, who sent you to ask the LORD, 'The LORD, the God of Israel, says this about the words that you heard:

**27** "Your heart was sensitive, and you made yourself humble in front of God. You heard his words against this place and against its people. You made yourself humble in front of me, tore your clothes, and cried in front of me. Thus I heard you," says the LORD.

**28** "Look, I will let you die and join your ancestors. You will be buried in peace. You will not see all the disaster that I will bring on this place and on its people."'" Then the men took her answer back to the king.

**29** Then the king sent messengers and collected all the elders of Judah and Jerusalem.

**30** The king went up to the house of the LORD. All the men of Judah, the people of Jerusalem, the priests, the Levites, and all the people, from the least important to the most important, went with him. The king read to them all the words of the book of the covenant that Hilkiah found in the house of the LORD.

**31** The king stood in his place. He made a covenant in front of the LORD. He agreed to follow the LORD and to obey his commands, his laws, and his rules with all his heart and all his soul. He agreed to obey the words of the covenant in this book.

**32** Then he made all the people in Jerusalem and Benjamin agree to the covenant. The people of Jerusalem obeyed the covenant of God, the God of their ancestors.

**33** Josiah removed all the disgusting idols from all the land of the Israelites. He made all the people in Israel serve the LORD their God. While Josiah lived, they did not stop obeying the LORD, the God of their ancestors.

## Chapter 35

**1** Josiah held a Passover for the LORD in Jerusalem. They killed the Passover lambs on the fourteenth day of the first month.

**2** He put the priests in their positions and encouraged them in the service of the house of the LORD.

**3** He said to the Levites who taught all Israel and who were holy to the LORD, "Put the holy ark in the house that Solomon, the son of David, the king of Israel, built. You do not have to carry the ark on your shoulders. Now serve the LORD your God and his people Israel.

**4** Prepare yourselves by your families and by your divisions. Obey the written rules of David, the king of Israel, and of his son Solomon.

**5** Stand in the holy place in groups. Each group of Levites must help a group of families of the people.

**6** Kill the Passover lambs and make yourselves holy. Prepare the lambs for your relatives. Do what the LORD commanded through Moses."

**7** Josiah gave to the people 30,000 lambs and young goats from his own flocks for the Passover offerings. He also gave 3,000 bulls. He gave these animals to all the people who were there. These animals were from the possessions of the king.

**8** His officials also gave willingly to the people, to the priests, and to the Levites. Hilkiah, Zechariah, and Jehiel, the officials in charge of the house of God, gave the priests 2,600 lambs and young goats and 300 oxen for the Passover offerings.

**9** Conaniah and his brothers Shemaiah and Nethaneel, and Hashabiah, Jeiel, and Jozabad, the leaders of the Levites, also gave animals. They gave the Levites 5,000 lambs and young goats and 500 oxen for the Passover offerings.

**10** Thus the service was ready. The priests stood in their places, and the Levites stood in their divisions, as the king commanded.

**11** They killed the Passover lambs. The priests put the blood on the altar, and the Levites removed the skins of the animals.

**12** They put aside the burnt offerings to give them to the groups of families of the people. The people offered them to the LORD, as the book of Moses tells. They did the same with the oxen.

**13** They roasted the Passover lambs over the fire, as the law told. They boiled the other holy offerings in pots, kettles, and pans. They quickly gave the meat to all the people.

**14** After this, the Levites prepared the meat for themselves and for the priests. The priests, the descendants of Aaron, offered the burnt offerings and the fat until night. Thus the Levites prepared meat for themselves and for the priests, the descendants of Aaron.

**15** The singers, the descendants of Asaph, were in their places, as David, Asaph, Heman, and Jeduthun the seer of the king commanded. The gatekeepers stayed at each gate. They did not have to leave their posts, because their relatives the Levites prepared the meat for them.

**16** Thus all the service of the LORD was ready on that day. They held the Passover and offered burnt offerings on the altar of the LORD, as King Josiah commanded.

**17** The Israelites who were there held the Passover at that time. They also held the Feast of Unleavened Bread for 7 days.

**18** Nobody held a Passover like it in Israel since the time of Samuel the prophet. None of the kings of Israel held a Passover like the Passover of Josiah. He held it with the priests, the Levites, all Judah and Israel who were there, and the people of Jerusalem.

**19** They held this Passover in the eighteenth year of the rule of Josiah.

**20** After Josiah prepared the temple, Necho, the king of Egypt, went to fight at Carchemish on the Euphrates River. Josiah went out to fight against him.

**21** But Necho sent messengers to Josiah. They said, "King of Judah, this war is not your problem. I do not come to attack you today. I come to attack the nation that is at war with me. God told me to go quickly. God is with me. Stop your opposition to God, or he will destroy you."

**22** But Josiah did not turn away from Necho. Josiah changed his clothes so that people did not know him, and he went to fight. He did not listen to the words that God spoke through Necho. He went to fight in the Valley of Megiddo.

**23** Archers shot arrows at King Josiah. The king said to his servants, "Take me away, because I am badly wounded."

**24** Thus his servants took him out of his chariot. They put him in his second chariot and brought him to Jerusalem. There he died. The people buried him in the tombs of his ancestors. All Judah and Jerusalem mourned for Josiah.

**25** Jeremiah wrote songs of grief for Josiah. To this day, all the male and female singers sing about Josiah in their songs of grief. These songs became a custom in Israel. They are written in the book of Laments.

**26** The other actions of Josiah and his good deeds in obedience to the law of the LORD

**27** and all his actions, from the start to the end, are in the book of the kings of Israel and Judah.

## Chapter 36

**1** Then the people of the land took Jehoahaz, the son of Josiah. They made him king in Jerusalem after his father.

**2** Jehoahaz was 23 years old when he became king. He ruled for 3 months in Jerusalem.

**3** The king of Egypt removed him from the throne in Jerusalem. He made the land pay a tax of 100 talents of silver and 1 talent of gold.

**4** The king of Egypt made Eliakim, the brother of Jehoahaz, king over Judah and Jerusalem. He changed the name of Eliakim to Jehoiakim. Necho took Jehoahaz, the brother of Eliakim, to Egypt.

**5** Jehoiakim was 25 years old when he became king. He ruled for 11 years in Jerusalem. He did evil in the sight of the LORD his God.

**6** Nebuchadnezzar, the king of Babylon, attacked Jehoiakim. He put bronze chains on him to take him to Babylon.

**7** Nebuchadnezzar also took some items from the house of the LORD to Babylon. He put them in his temple in Babylon.

**8** The other actions of Jehoiakim, the disgusting things that he did, and the evil in him are in the book of the kings of Israel and Judah. Jehoiachin his son became king after him.

**9** Jehoiachin was 8 years old when he became king. He ruled for 3 months and 10 days in Jerusalem. He did evil in the sight of the LORD.

**10** In the spring, King Nebuchadnezzar sent men and brought Jehoiachin to Babylon. He also took the valuable items from the house of the LORD. He made Zedekiah, the relative of Jehoiachin, king over Judah and Jerusalem.

**11** Zedekiah was 21 years old when he became king. He ruled for 11 years in Jerusalem.

**12** He did evil in the sight of the LORD his God. Jeremiah the prophet spoke the words of the LORD to him. But Zedekiah did not make himself humble in front of Jeremiah.

**13** Zedekiah also rebelled against King Nebuchadnezzar. Nebuchadnezzar made him promise loyalty with an oath in the name of God. But Zedekiah was stubborn. He refused to turn back to the LORD, the God of Israel.

**14** Also, all the leaders of the priests and the people became more and more unfaithful. They did all the disgusting things of the other nations. They made the house of the LORD unclean. The LORD made this house holy in Jerusalem.

**15** The LORD, the God of their ancestors, sent his messengers to them again and again. He did this because he had pity on his people and on his house.

**16** But they made fun of the messengers of God. They hated his words and laughed at his prophets. Finally the anger of the LORD came on his people, and nothing could stop it.

**17** Thus the LORD brought the king of the Babylonians against them. The king killed their young men with the sword in the holy house. He had no pity on young men or young women, on the old or the weak. God gave all of them into the power of the king.

**18** The king of Babylon took to Babylon all the items of the house of God, large and small. He took the treasures of the house of the LORD and the treasures of the king and of his officials.

**19** The Babylonians burned the house of God and broke down the wall of Jerusalem. They burned all the palaces and destroyed all the valuable items.

**20** The king took the people who escaped the sword to Babylon. They became servants to him and his sons until the kingdom of Persia started to rule.

**21** Thus the word of the LORD through Jeremiah became true. The land had rest for its Sabbaths. During all the time that the land was empty, it had rest, until 70 years were complete.

**22** In the first year of Cyrus, the king of Persia, the LORD made true the word that he spoke through Jeremiah. The LORD caused Cyrus, the king of Persia, to send a message through all his kingdom. Cyrus also wrote the message. It said,

**23** "Cyrus, the king of Persia, says this: 'The LORD, the God of heaven, gave me all the kingdoms of the earth. He told me to build a house for him in Jerusalem, in Judah. Any of his people among you can go up to Jerusalem. May the LORD his God be with him.'"
